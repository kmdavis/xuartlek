# The Book

The campaign's spine, and the one page a guest GM needs before running an arc.
The Factor herself is in [[Connie Underfoot]]. How the party got here is in
[[statement-of-account]].

---

## The one-paragraph version

The party owe Constance Underfoot 12,000 gp. She holds their paper, she owns
their airship, and she rents out their time to the highest bidder until the
number reaches zero. Four arcs of three sessions clears it, which is one arc per
player in the GM chair, and lands them at 5th level and free. Each incoming GM is
the winning bidder.

---

## The rule that governs everything

**All political consequence resolves at the broker, never at the party.**

The players asked for no political gameplay. They are content to work for
political entities; they do not want to play politics. So if a job embarrasses a
Great House, that is Connie's problem to smooth over. The only thing the party
ever see of it is next month's work being worse paid or nastier.

Politics is weather, expressed entirely through the job board. Nobody is ever
asked whose side they are on.

---

## How an arc starts

The clerk, Ambrose Sowerby, posts a lot. Bidders make offers. The crew are told
where to be and when.

**You are the winning bidder.** Turn up, describe your client in three sentences,
run your arc. You do not need to clear anything with anyone, you do not need to
know what any other GM is doing, and your client can be anybody on any world.

The party do not choose the job and cannot decline it. They are never told who the
client is -- same rule, opposite reason to the one people assume: if you do not
know who hired you, nobody can make you say. Connie tells crews that to their
faces.

---

## The two legs

A job runs in two parts, and this gives you a free three-act structure.

| Leg | Route | Speed | Paperwork |
|---|---|---|---|
| **People** | Ring. One person and what they can carry | Instant | None at all |
| **Freight** | The public network, anchor to anchor | Weeks | Full. Registered hull, manifest, tolls |

Connie leases **person-gauge** portal rings. They pass one person and a pack, and
need no hardware waiting on the far side, so she can put a crew on any world in
the empire or off the edge of it with no record that anybody travelled.

Ship-gauge transit needs an anchor at **both** ends, which is why the empire's
registered network still matters and why freight still moves slowly and legally.

So the shape of nearly every arc is:

1. The crew go through the ring with what they can carry. No armoury, no ship, no
   way home.
2. They do the job undermanned and underequipped. This is where the drama is.
3. The *Grim Zephyr* arrives by the legal route, weeks later, on schedule, and the
   tone of the arc turns.

**The return is a reservation, confirmed on signal, not a door they can open.**
Getting home is a decision somebody in Lapurrak makes about them. Normally she
always confirms it. Let the players learn this as logistics; it is years before it
means anything else.

## The money

### The Book

Opens at **12,000 gp**. That is what Connie paid at the debtors' market (6,000)
plus her stated margin (double), and it is 35% of the 34,261 gp the empire
assessed. She will tell the party all of that, unprompted, at the first meeting.
She will not say what she paid. That spread is her margin and she is straight
about having one.

**She never lies about the number.** Show them the arithmetic every single arc:
costs, fee, her cut, remainder, new balance.

### Per-arc costs, charged to the Book

| Item | gp |
|---|---|
| Two person-gauge ring openings, out and return, at 400 | 800 |
| *Grim Zephyr* portal tolls, four transits at 100 | 400 |
| **Total, added to the Book before anyone is paid** | **1,200** |

**Read the toll line out loud every arc.** They were destroyed by unpaid tolls, and
now they pay tolls, on time, forever, and it goes on their tab. Every transit is an
Officer of Transit at a window, doing the paperwork correctly, and never once making
an error in their favour.

### The schedule

Base fees escalate as the crew becomes worth more. Connie's commission is 25%.

| Arc | Party level | Client fee | Her cut | Costs | Net off the Book | Balance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | -- | **12,000** |
| 1 | 3 | 3,600 | 900 | 1,200 | 1,500 | 10,500 |
| 2 | 3 | 5,200 | 1,300 | 1,200 | 2,700 | 7,800 |
| 3 | 4 | 6,800 | 1,700 | 1,200 | 3,900 | 3,900 |
| 4 | 5 | 8,400 | 2,100 | 1,200 | 5,100 | **clear, +1,200** |

They clear it on the last job, at 5th level, in the fourth arc, after everyone has
had the chair once, and they walk away with 1,200 gp of their own even on a
straight Acceptable run.

### Reputation is a price, not a relationship

Work cleanly and the crew fetch more next time. Make a mess and they fetch less.
There is no faction standing, no favour economy and nobody to keep sweet. Just a
figure that moves. This also gives you a scoring rubric you do not have to invent:
grade the arc, apply the multiplier, tell the players the number.

| Band | Multiplier | After four arcs |
|---|---|---|
| Exemplary | x1.25 | Free, and **5,700 gp in hand** |
| Clean | x1.1 | Free, and **3,000 gp in hand** |
| Acceptable | x1.0 | Free, and **1,200 gp in hand** |
| Messy | x0.85 | Still owes 1,500 gp |
| Disaster | x0.7 | Still owes 4,200 gp |

Nobody gets trapped. The worst case is two more arcs, and the best case is freedom
with a stake. That surplus is the first money that has ever actually belonged to
them.

## The hard rule: the Book never touches their loot

**This is the one that breaks the game if you get it wrong.**

PF2E's Table 10-9 gives a party of four **2,700 gp of treasure across levels 3, 4
and 5** (500 + 850 + 1,350). That is their gear budget and it stays entirely
theirs. Loot found on a mission is loot. The client fee is a separate economy that
exists only on Connie's ledger.

If you let the Book take a cut of their treasure, they fall behind the wealth curve
and every GM after you inherits an underequipped party. Don't.

**The one place the two economies touch, and it is the players' choice:** they may
pay their own money into the Book. Roughly 500 gp buys back a bit over half an
arc. Less gear now, freedom sooner. That is the only trade in the design and it
should always be offered, never suggested.

## Pacing

Four arcs, three sessions each, twelve sessions.

| XP per level | Level 3 to 5 | Per session over twelve |
|---|---|---|
| 1,000 (RAW) | 2,000 XP | 167 XP |
| 800 (as the sheets are configured) | 1,600 XP | 133 XP |

At the group's real cadence that is roughly a year of play. If that is too long,
cut arcs to two sessions and run the eight-session route, but XP per session has
to climb to 250 and that is hot.

---

## What clearing the Book actually gets them

- **The registry.** Connie re-registered the *Grim Zephyr* in her own name; clearing
  the Book transfers it to them. Freedom is a document.
- **The surplus**, if they earned one.
- **A card in the drawer.** She keeps it. That is the point of keeping it, and her
  long game is that they take her calls afterwards anyway.

---

## Things a guest GM should not do

1. **Do not let Connie solve the problem.** Her leverage is commercial, not
   magical. When a scene invites "why doesn't she just fix this," the answer is
   that fixing it costs more than it is worth, or that her landlord will not wear
   it.
2. **Do not make her threaten anyone.** She has no muscle and has never needed
   any. If a crew refuses work or fails badly, she sells their card to somebody who
   does keep muscle, and she will say so plainly and without heat.
3. **Do not move the Book's opening figure.** If the players ever see the number
   move for reasons other than the arithmetic, the whole device dies.
4. **Do not ask the party to pick a side.** See the rule at the top.
5. **Do not kill the crew from Session 10.** They went in the same lot with smaller
   books of their own, worked off against wages. They are not on the party's
   balance and they are not yours to spend.
