**Full Name**: Captain Jonas Stormeye
**Status**: Deceased (destroyed)
**Former Affiliation**: Independent ship captain, Sielmoro

## Description

In life, Jonas Stormeye was a rugged sea captain with dark hair and a thick beard, typically dressed in a burgundy coat and tricorn hat. He had the weathered look of a man who'd spent decades on the water--capable, driven, perhaps too ambitious for his own good.

In undeath, he became something terrible: a wraith-like guardian wreathed in dark ethereal energy, his eyes glowing with unnatural light--one blue, one burning orange. He wielded a curved spectral blade and commanded the shades of his own slaughtered crew.

**Ancestry**: Human (Male)
**Role**: Ship Captain → Cursed Guardian (undead)

## The Curse

Captain Stormeye led 11 of his crew from Sielmoro into the jungle, seeking ancient ruins rumored to hold treasure. What they found was something far older and more dangerous.

When Stormeye touched a cursed artifact within the ruins, he was instantly transformed into a wraith-like creature--bound to that place as its guardian. In his new form, he slaughtered his own crew. Those he killed rose as spectres, adding to the ruins' defenses.

The curse anchored him to the ruins; he could not pursue those who fled.

## The Survivors

Five of Stormeye's crew escaped the initial massacre but became trapped--the door sealed behind them, and their transformed captain blocked any advance. They survived in that liminal space until the party arrived on a rescue mission.

## GM Notes

Captain Stormeye represents the dangers of greed and the unknown horrors waiting in ancient places. His transformation was instant and complete--a warning about cursed artifacts.

The ruins themselves may still hold secrets. The curse that created Stormeye had a source, and that source may not have been fully neutralized.

Consider:
- What was the original purpose of the ruins?
- Who placed the curse, and why?
- Are there other such sites in the region?
- Did destroying Stormeye truly end the curse, or merely its guardian?
