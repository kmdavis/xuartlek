## The Ship: Grim Zephyr

An **amphibious freighter**. Her design began as a bulk freighter and was then compromised
heavily to make her seaworthy, so that she can settle into an ordinary water harbour and
work her cargo at any deep berth. A true bulk freighter is far too heavy to set down at
all, and needs a port fitted with cargo handling towers; the Grim Zephyr needs nothing but
a deep berth. That flexibility cost her roughly two thirds of a bulk freighter's lift.

She has **no cargo doors in her sides**. Ports in the hull would ruin the seaworthiness
the whole design was bought at, and tramp and bulk freighters can afford them precisely
because they never float. Instead she loads **vertically**: a line of cargo trunks runs
straight up through every deck from the cargo deck to the top deck, and a crane amidships
hoists loads up the trunks and sets them down on the top deck, where a pier crane can take
them, or the crew can do it the old fashioned way.

See [[Naval Architecture]] for how rings, lift and portal gauge
work across the fleet.

### Ring and Rig

One single unbroken hoop, 95 ft across, encircling the whole ship in a transverse plane,
held off the planking by **eight** radial spars lying flat in the ring's own plane. Eight is
the men-o-war end of the usual range, and she carries them because everything she weighs
hangs on this one hoop.

**She sits at the bottom of her ring, not in the middle of it.** The hoop's lower arc runs
close beneath the keel, reaching barely a hand's breadth below it, so the ring rises almost
entirely above her. That makes the hoop part of her landing gear and the first thing to
touch in a grounding. Its upper arc stands high above her weather deck, the tallest
structure on the ship after the mainmast.

**The ring never enters the hull.** It stands wholly outside the planking, held by its eight
spars, which pass through braced brass collars in the hull sides. Power reaches it through
lagged conduits run from the engine out along the spars. Nothing else connects ship to
ring.

The hoop is faired as an aerofoil for the sake of the air, and the arc that goes underwater
is bronze-sheathed and stout rather than fine: she has no need of speed through water, so it
is built to survive a knock, not to cut. It carries no fouling at all, at any depth. The
power running through the iron kills anything that tries to settle.

The deck plans appear to show two separate ring assemblies, one to each side. That is only
an artefact of slicing a vertical hoop horizontally, deck by deck. It is one continuous
ring.

Three masts, and no ventral mast:

- **Mainmast**, vertical on the centreline at about half her length aft of the bow
  (~103 ft), well clear of the ring. Telescopes into its own stump to strike.
- **Two lateral masts**, port and starboard, stepped **on the hull at main deck level**,
  low down and well below the weather deck, not on the mainmast or anywhere aloft. They
  project outboard and slightly aft, almost horizontal. Each carries an enormous wing sail
  on a long tapering leading-edge spar. Tip to tip the pair span 245 ft, wider than she is
  long. Both sweep aft on hinged tabernacles to strike.

Struck for a portal transit, nothing on her projects outside the 95 ft circle of her own
ring, with 12.5 ft of clearance all round in a 120 ft aperture.

### Armament and fields of fire

She is a merchant, and her armament says so. **Every heavy weapon she carries is a chase
piece**, bearing along the keel line: one forward, two aft. She has **no broadside at all**.
Anything that gets on her beam is safe from everything but light crossbows on the rails.

| Position | Deck | Weapon | When afloat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fore chase, upper | Main deck | **1 heavy arbalest** | dry, usable |
| Fore chase, lower | Lower deck | 3 light crossbows | **submerged** |
| After chase, upper | Top deck | **1 heavy arbalest** | barely above the water; unusable in any sea |
| After chase, lower | Main deck | **1 heavy arbalest** | **submerged** |
| Rails | Top deck | a handful of light crossbows on swivels | dry, usable |

**Two arbalests aft against one forward: she is built to shake off a pursuer, not to pursue.**
For a hull designed to carry cargo rather than to fight, that is the right trade. What her
current owners do with her is another matter.

**Afloat, she is very nearly disarmed.** Three of her four heavy positions are underwater or
awash the moment she settles: the forward crossbow trio is submerged outright, the lower after
arbalest with it, and the upper after arbalest sits so close to the surface that any real sea
puts it out of action. What is left is one forward arbalest, one doubtful arbalest astern, and
the rail crossbows. This is a strong reason never to set down outside a harbour, and a
standing invitation to anyone who knows her trim.

#### Closing the drowned ports

Her three forward crossbow ports and her lower after arbalest port all go under when she
floats. Each is built as a bronze-lined bore with a faired sliding **outer shutter** flush in
the planking and a heavy **inner door** on wedge-cam dogs, and it is the inner door that keeps
the sea out. See [[Naval Architecture|Ports below the waterline]]
for why.

The drill before every landing, and it is on the mate:

1. Unload and run the weapon inboard on its slide.
2. Swing the inner door and set all four dogs.
3. Drop the outer shutter and drive its wedges.
4. Crack the drain cock and see what comes out.

Miss step four and the first anyone knows of a bad shutter is water on the lower deck.

### Capabilities and limits

- **Flies, floats and sails.** She has a rudder and tiller on a proper sternpost, because
  she has to handle on water. The point is making port, not cruising oceans.
- **No landing legs.** Her ring is her landing gear, and a single hoop is a single line of
  contact, so she cannot stand on it alone -- she would tip fore or aft. Water solves it, and
  that is a large part of why she was built to float at all.
- **A grounding takes the ring, not the hull.** The hoop's lower arc sits a hand's breadth
  below her keel, so it meets any bottom first. Cheaper than the alternative, and still a
  shipyard job.
- **Single ring, no redundancy.** Lose it and she is a boat, which is exactly why she was
  built seaworthy.
- **Armed with arbalests, not cannon,** and chase pieces only. See
  [Armament and fields of fire](#armament-and-fields-of-fire) above and
  [[House Rules & Rulings|Ship Weapons]].
- **She is not built for speed on water, only for handling in a harbour.** She descends
  into the water inside the harbour or just outside it, then warps slowly and carefully up
  to the pier on her own lines and capstans.
- **Speed** ~4 mph, 4 hexes per day. See [[House Rules & Rulings|Travel Pace]].
