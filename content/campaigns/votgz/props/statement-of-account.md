# Statement of Account

Two documents. The first is what Pender Holst is holding **right now**, in the
Dusk Stalker den, at the moment session 20 opens. The second arrives about three
months later and ends the party's independence.

Both are player-facing. Hand them over as printed props if you can.

---

## Document 1 -- Pender's demand

He crossed a sea to serve this. He will read every line. He waives nothing.

```
         PRINCIPALITY OF ZOLNUTH -- REVENUE OFFICE, SIELMORO
         DEMAND FOR PAYMENT
         Re: prize sloop, sold at Sielmoro
         Sellers: the crew of the vessel presently styled GRIM ZEPHYR
         Serving officer: P. HOLST, Revenue Officer (Second Class)

   1  Prize share, Principality of Zolnuth,
         one-third of prize sold (700 gp) .....................  233 gp 4 sp
   2  Vessel transfer duty, prize sloop,
         assessed value 680 gp at 1/200 .......................    3 gp 4 sp
   3  Collection fee, Revenue Office, 5% ......................   11 gp 7 sp
   4  Late penalty, 30 days at 1 gp/day .......................   30 gp

                                     TOTAL DUE ................  278 gp 5 sp
```

They sold the sloop for 700 gp and netted 663 after repairs. This is **42% of
everything they made on it.**

### Notes for the GM

**Line 1 is retroactive and legal.** The prize was taken before the Letter of
Marque was issued, so the sale was not privateering. The Principality's remedy is
to treat the commission as covering it, in exchange for the standard one-third.
That is not a favour, it is the price of the sale being lawful at all. Refusing to
pay does not make the bill go away; it makes the sale piracy.

**Line 2 is what the players already heard.** In session 18 Pender named 3 gp 4
sp and a 2 gp late penalty. That was true and it was line 2 of four. Combat
started before he got through the rest. Nobody misheard anything.

**The Office assessed the hull at 680 gp**, not the 700 they actually got.
Nobody asked them.

**He does not work for the empire.** Pender is a revenue officer for the Principality
of Zolnuth, out of Sielmoro. Local government, local duties, local prize shares. The
imperial Authority is something he files reports to, not something he belongs to.

**And he does not mention the other thing.** Opening their file to collect this is
how he found the *Grim Zephyr*: logged inbound at Issoire, never logged out, registry
unresolved. That is not a Zolnuth matter, so he did the correct thing and **reported
it upward to the Authority** before he left. The derelict flag is already raised and
Provident Salvage is already coming. It is out of his hands and he will not raise it.

A parish clerk's thoroughness is what put an imperial body onto them.

Pender is not cruel and not corrupt. He is *correct*. He would be genuinely
distressed by what happens next and entirely unable to see that he did anything
wrong, because he didn't.

## How to run the reveal

**The toll was one hundred gold.** Put line 5 in front of them and let it sit.
Everything above and below it exists because nobody stopped at the window on the
way through, in a panic, with a stolen ship, eleven months ago.

**Lines 14 and 15.** The Office charges them to raise the derelict flag and
charges them again to take it down.

**Line 18.** They are billed 66 gp for a *Zolnuth* revenue officer's travel and
subsistence while he came to the jungle to serve them Document 1. The Authority did
not send him. It passes the charge through anyway.

**Line 8 is canon.** They renamed the *Verdant Zephyr*. The registry knows the
hull by its old name, which is why the header reads UNRESOLVED.

**Line 26 is the one they never saw coming.** Provident Salvage quoted 900 gp for
the tow and meant it. The statutory salvage award is a claim against the vessel
filed with the Office, not a negotiation with the owner, and it crystallises when
a flagged derelict is *delivered*. Accepting the reasonable offer is what
triggered the unreasonable one. Asked afterwards whether he knew, the salvor says
yes, of course, and is not being cruel. It did not occur to him that they didn't
know, because everyone knows.

**Nobody colluded.** The salvor was honest, the yard was honest, the Office was
correct, and Pender was diligent. There is no villain in this document, which is
what makes it work.

**Halloran is the only party who behaved well.** His invoice is the smallest thing
on the page, his price was fair, and it made no difference to anything.

## Aftermath

Ship and crew were seized and sold at the debtors' market. The sale was a
**judicial sale under Office authority**, which passes clean title free of all
prior liens and encumbrances, and it **discharged the imperial account**. The
party do not owe 34,261 gp to anybody any more.

| | gp | |
|---|---|---|
| Face value | 34,261 | discharged by the sale |
| Winning bid, ship and crew as one lot | 6,000 | 17.5% of face |
| **The Book, owed to the Factor** | **12,000** | 35% of face, **6.7x their net worth** |

Implied hull value, from the statutory salvage award: **24,000 gp**.

See [[Connie Underfoot]] for the Factor, and the Book arc file for how the debt is
worked off.

### Title, so nobody has to relitigate it

The original owner of the *Verdant Zephyr* has no claim. They were made whole by
their underwriters, who took the title by subrogation, declared a constructive
total loss and struck the registration -- which is why the hull that surfaced at
Issoire matched no live registry entry. And even if they had a claim, the judicial
sale extinguished it.

**Connie re-registers her.** The *Grim Zephyr* becomes a properly registered
imperial hull with a named owner of record, which is how she can transit portals
lawfully at 100 gp a time. Clearing the Book transfers the registry to the party.
Freedom is a document.

**What survives instead of a lien.** Underwriters investigate losses and keep
files forever. Somewhere there is a claim file on the *Verdant Zephyr* containing
statements from a helmsman, two cooks and a longshoreman, describing the people
who came out of the cargo. Nobody has cross-referenced it against the crew of a
freighter recently sold at the debtors' market.

A cross-reference is what started all of this.
