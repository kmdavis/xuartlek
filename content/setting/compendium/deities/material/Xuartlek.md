# Xuartlek

## The short version

Xuartlek is many things. First, Xuartlek was a man. Well. A Tortle. A **Skyy
Tortle**. Xuartlek was, and arguably still is, an Archmage. Xuartlek is also a city.

How is that possible? Skyy Tortles never stop growing, so an ancient one gets to be
quite, quite large. A mile long is fairly normal. They also slow down as they age,
so these ancient behemoths are more or less stationary, and younger Skyy Tortles
have a habit of building villages on their backs.

Fine, you say. A village, on a very old, very large, very senile tortle. But you
said **city**.

Xuartlek was not a normal Tortle. He had a bit of an ego, which you rather need if
you intend to become an Archmage, and he knew exactly what was coming for him. He
spent his waning years researching how to escape it: how to keep his mind, and how
to keep moving.

He **almost** succeeded.

His experiments made him grow far larger and far faster than any Skyy Tortle should.
And he *sort of* kept his wits about him.

His shell is now about twenty-five miles long, carries roughly a million souls, and
is the seat of an empire of sorts spread across a dozen worlds.

## Titles

The Shell That Walks Between Worlds. Bearer of the High City. The Mind Untethered.
The Wandering Metropolis. The Eternal Tortle. Great Shell of Empire.

## Edicts and anathema

**Edict.** Pursue knowledge relentlessly, build lasting communities, bridge worlds
through trade.

**Anathema.** Accept mental decline, abandon those who depend on you, cease growing.

## The ritual that half worked

Born on [[Tortleheim]] in **-125**. Archmage by **-075**. While other Skyy Tortles
accepted growth and mental decline with stoic grace, he raged against it, and his
metabolism ran hot on constant magical experimentation.

As the fog first reached him he attempted his great work: a ritual to separate his
mind from his flesh while keeping the connection. He performed it on the highest
peak of Tortleheim as his hundredth year approached, in **-025**.

It went both terribly wrong and wonderfully right. His body erupted beyond every
natural limit. His mind was not preserved but scattered and reformed into something
that exists partly outside his body. The transformation should have killed him.
Instead it made him the first Skyy Tortle to achieve divinity, the first large enough
to carry a city, and the first whose mind stayed sharp while becoming alien to
everyone else's.

Six hundred and twenty-five years later he is still looking for the rest of the
answer: not immortality, which he has, but the reunion of mind and body that still
eludes him.

**Four adolescents in the Under City got it by accident.** See [[Government of Xuartlek]].

## The exodus

The Skyy Tortles declared a new era in **000** and he departed Tortleheim in **005**.
Several thousand went with him and built a city on his shell. This was **not a flight
from catastrophe.** It was a voluntary exodus: pilgrims following a partially
ascended leader into the unknown, and everything about how the city governs itself
still follows from that.

They removed the leap days from their calendar, because they were no longer orbiting
anything and could never go home.

## What he is like

He embodies the paradox of vastness and precision: a body spanning miles, a mind that
still delights in the smallest detail of arcane theory. Patient by necessity, since
each step takes hours. Driven by an urgency only he understands.

Warm toward the small beings living on him. He regards them as partners in the
experiment rather than as parasites, favours scholars and innovators, and expresses
approval through beneficial city-planning "accidents". His sense of humour is
cosmic: a misplaced building, a street that loops back on itself, gentle reminders
that the ground is alive and paying attention.

His greatest fear is stagnation. His greatest joy is discovery. He is generous with
wisdom and cryptic in delivery, communicating through dreams, architectural
symbolism, and the sudden appearance of exactly the right book.

## Speaking

He is officially head of state and almost entirely silent. When he does speak, every
soul within a hundred kilometres hears it, and it is an event of tremendous
significance that happens perhaps once in a generation.

Everything the government does is technically delegated authority, and delegation
from a silent god can never be confirmed. That is the structural reason no
institution in the city has ever been able to claim the last word.

> *The voice comes from everywhere and nowhere, each word taking minutes to form.*
>
> "Ahhhh... visitors. Or perhaps... residents? The distinction... grows less
> clear... with each... passing century. I am... Xuartlek. I was... Xuartlek. I will
> be... Xuartlek. The shell you stand upon... the city you inhabit... the mind that
> speaks... all one... yet not... one.
>
> But tell me... small quick thing... what is it... to have... a mind and body...
> that still... speak as one? I have... forgotten... yet remember... remembering."

## Stories told about him

> When Xuartlek first arrived at Khashayar, the desert tried to swallow him whole.
> Sandstorms raged for seven days. On the eighth, the city on his shell had opened
> trade with three nomad tribes and an oasis had formed in his footprints.

> During the Convergence of the Five Moons over Tertara, his mind is said to have
> rejoined his body for exactly one hour. In that time he spoke a single word that
> revolutionised magical theory across three schools. Scholars still argue about
> what it was, because every witness heard something different.

> The merchant princes of Qigang once tried to claim the High City under an ancient
> law of salvage. He rolled over in his sleep, rearranging the entire city and
> creating a new district that spelled out NO in the old tongue when seen from
> above.

> During the Plague of Forgetting on Emerraine he opened his deepest shell chambers,
> revealing libraries that predated the plague. The knowledge saved the world. Those
> who went in speak of hearing whispers in languages not yet invented.

> He once dreamed so deeply that his consciousness touched the Far Realms. The dream
> lasted moments. The city gained three impossible buildings that exist in more
> dimensions than architecture should allow, and they remain its most prestigious
> addresses.

## Relations

- **Gwenlliant**, a fellow city-builder with entirely different methods.
- **Hassan Opendoor**, trade partner. One opens doors; one carries cities through
