## Description

In a world where water is worth more than gold and days last a week, an ocean-covered planet hangs tantalizingly in the sky--close enough to see its storms, forever out of reach, cursed with monsters that hunger as eternally as the deserts below thirst.

## Full Description

Khashayar is a world defined by extremes. Days stretch for 180 hours--long enough for the sun to bake the sands white-hot and turn stone into cracked mosaics of heat stress. Nights last equally long, plunging temperatures so low that frost forms on dunes and unprepared travelers freeze in their sleep. Between these extremes, roughly 30 hours of tolerable twilight at dawn and dusk offer the only opportunities for outdoor survival.

Life revolves around the precious twilight hours--these are when all outdoor work must happen. Fields need tending, buildings need repair, caravans must travel, and markets must bustle with activity. Everyone able to work outdoors does so during twilight, pushing themselves to stay awake for most of the 30-hour window, sleeping only at its beginning and end. The dangerous periods--those long 60-hour stretches of killing heat and deadly cold--belong to indoor life. Most inhabitants divide these blocks into three wake periods separated by sleep, conducting indoor business, attending schools and theaters, working in covered markets and workshops. Eight sleep periods per 180-hour cycle become normal; children learn this rhythm from birth.

But the outdoors cannot be entirely abandoned during the extremes. Ifrit work the day-scorched fields and guard posts, their fire-touched nature making them invaluable during the blazing hours when others would die within minutes. They rest during twilight while everyone else labors. Undine, water-touched and cold-resistant, serve the same role during deep night, maintaining watch over frost-covered crops and frozen streets while their breath turns to ice crystals. Both earn elite status and higher wages for doing what others cannot--surviving the unsurvivable.

Agriculture clings to the rivers like ancient Egypt, with irrigation canals extending perhaps a mile or two from the water's edge. At twilight's end, workers drape fields with ingenious mesh covers: translucent fabric for day that admits some light while blocking killing heat and trapping precious humidity; thicker insulating cloth for night that preserves warmth against the freeze. This twice-daily ritual of covering and uncovering fields employs hundreds and marks the transition between the world's moods as clearly as any clock. Water is rationed with religious precision--every citizen knows their daily allocation, and theft of water carries the same penalty as murder.

But the cruelest irony hangs in the sky. Nushirvan dominates the heavens as a massive indigo disc, visible day and night as it traces its ten-day orbit. The brother-king drowned beneath infinite oceans as punishment for his betrayal--pure water, endless water, enough water to drown Khashayar's every desert ten times over. Yet that water offers no salvation. The curse that transformed Nushirvan populated his waters with creatures beyond mortal understanding, monsters that hunger eternally just as Khashayar thirsts eternally. Water so close you can see its storms with naked eyes, yet as unreachable as redemption itself.

The people of Khashayar long ago learned to live with this duality: blessed with a companion world, cursed by its uselessness. Perhaps this is why they value what can be held over what can merely be seen--tangible water over distant oceans, real loyalty over ancient brotherhood, survival over mythology.

## Naming Conventions

Persian/Zoroastrian mythology throughout:
* **Shamsara**: Star name (Persian "shams" = sun)
* **Khashayar**: Old Persian form of Xerxes (Khshayarsha), one of the cursed brother-kings
* **Nushirvan**: Anushirvan/Khosrow I "The Just Soul", the other brother-king
* **Mithrayan**: From Mithra/Mithras, ancient Persian solar deity (distant gas giant with seven divine moons)
* **Fereydun**: Legendary Persian king from the Shahnameh (outer world with three moons named for his sons)
* **Zahhakan**: From Zahhak, the dragon-king/tyrant of Persian mythology (cold outer world)
* Mithrayan's seven moons: The Amesha Spentas (seven divine emanations in Zoroastrianism)
* Fereydun's three moons: His three sons from Persian legend, two of whom murdered their brother

## Physical Characteristics

**Orbital Period:** ~310 Khashayari days (binary orbit with Nushirvan)
**Rotation Period:** 180 hours per day (7.5 Earth days)
**Diameter:** Standard rocky world
**Gravity:** Standard
**Atmosphere:** Breathable but dry

**Binary Planet System:**
* Khashayar and Nushirvan are true binary planets
* Both orbit their common barycenter in a 10-day period
* The entire binary system orbits Shamsara together
* Nushirvan appears in Khashayar's sky as a massive indigo disc filling ~1/4 of the sky
* Serves as the primary "moon" for calendar purposes

**Climate:**
* Extreme day/night temperature swings due to 180-hour rotation
* 30-hour twilight periods (tolerable): ALL outdoor work
* 60-hour peak day (lethal heat): Indoor activities, Ifrit work outside
* 30-hour dusk twilight (tolerable): Outdoor work resumes
* 60-hour deep night (lethal cold): Indoor activities, Undine work outside

## Geography

**No Continents:**
No oceans means no continental plates--geography organized by river valleys instead

**River Valleys:**
* ALL civilization within 1-2 miles of water sources
* Ancient Egypt parallel: Nile-like agriculture along rivers
* Irrigation canals extend the habitable zone
* Twice-daily covering ritual: Mesh for day (light-permeable, heat-blocking), thick cloth for night (insulation)

**White-Hot Deserts:**
* Between rivers: Lethal during day extremes
* Sands baked so hot they can ignite organic matter
* Travel only during twilight periods or with Ifrit guides

**Frozen Wastes:**
* Same deserts freeze solid during night extremes
* Frost forms on dunes, creating treacherous terrain
* Travel requires Undine guides or extreme cold-weather gear

## Astronomical Features

**Nushirvan (The Water World):**
* Binary companion planet, pure ocean from pole to pole
* Indigo coloration visible from Khashayar
* Fills approximately 1/4 of the sky
* Visible day and night
* Completes 10-day mutual orbit serving as calendar marker
* Has icy rings (possibly destroyed moon or captured cometary debris)
* "The tears Nushirvan cannot shed"--ring shadows occasionally visible

**The Brother-Kings Myth:**
In prehistory, Khashayar and Nushirvan were brother-kings ruling a unified empire. In simultaneous betrayal, each struck at the other at the exact same moment. The gods judged both guilty and transformed them into worlds:
* Khashayar: Cursed with endless desert and eternal thirst
* Nushirvan: Drowned beneath monster-filled oceans, forever deadly
* Bound in eternal 10-day orbit--neither can escape their shared guilt

**The Cruel Irony:**
Pure water, endless water, enough to drown every desert on Khashayar ten times over--hanging in the sky close enough to see storms with naked eyes. Yet completely unreachable, populated by monsters, offering no salvation. The people learn to value what can be held over what can merely be seen.

## Culture

**Species (Pathfinder 2E):**
* **Humans** (Skilled, Versatile--majority population)
* **Ifrit** (Ember, Brightflame--elite day laborers, heat-immune, high status and wages)
* **Undine** (Rime, Tide--elite night laborers, cold-resistant, high status and wages)
* **Amanzi** (Desert ancestry)
* **Aphorite** (Lawbringer heritage--enforce water law with religious fervor)
* **Nagaji** (Scaled ancestry)
* **Shabti** (Clay ancestry)

**Daily Rhythm (180-Hour Cycle):**
* **30-hour dawn twilight** (tolerable): ALL outdoor work, everyone mobilizes
* **60-hour peak day** (lethal heat): Indoor activities--schools, theaters, markets, workshops. Ifrit work outside.
* **30-hour dusk twilight** (tolerable): Outdoor work resumes, final push before night
* **60-hour deep night** (lethal cold): Indoor activities resume. Undine work outside.
* **Sleep**: 8 periods per cycle (once at start/end of each twilight, twice during each extreme)
* **Meals**: 2-3 during twilight (heavy), 6-9 more during indoor periods (light)
* Everyone pushes to stay awake during twilight--too precious to waste sleeping

**Water Law:**
* Water is currency, status, power--more valuable than gold
* Every citizen has daily allocation enforced by Aphorite water-judges
* Theft of water = murder (capital punishment)
* Laws older than the oldest empire
* Religious fervor surrounds water rights
* Complex irrigation law governs canal maintenance and distribution

**Agriculture:**
* All fields within 1-2 miles of rivers
* Twice-daily covering ritual: Community event marking transitions
* Translucent mesh for day: Allows light, blocks heat, traps humidity
* Thick cloth for night: Insulation against freezing
* Ifrit and Undine employed as field-watchers during extremes

**Magic Level:** Moderate
* Strong elemental/fire traditions
* Water divination highly developed
* Ifrit and Undine magic integrated into survival systems

**Technology Level:** Medieval
* Advanced water engineering: Irrigation, rationing systems, canal networks
* Clockwork timing mechanisms for covering schedules
* Architecture designed for extreme temperature management

**Theme:** Persian-inspired desert fantasy with grim survival elements

## History

**The Myth of the Brother-Kings:**
Ancient oral traditions speak of the unified empire before the gods' judgment. The exact nature of the betrayal varies by telling--some say it was over succession, others over a woman, still others over philosophical differences about governance. All agree on the simultaneity and the divine punishment.

**Cultural Foundation:**
The myth shapes Khashayari culture profoundly:
* Value of loyalty over ambition
* Suspicion of brotherly rivalry
* Emphasis on water-sharing as sacred trust
* Understanding that curses can bind victims together eternally

**Xuartlek Connection:**
* Two Low City districts: Dhiashrir, Ghonumi
* First glimpse of worlds where water is common--existentially shocking
* Trade: Water engineering expertise, fire-resistant crafts, desert survival gear, heat-management technology
* Most valuable import: Fresh water (extreme luxury good, almost religious significance)
* Cultural impact: Learning that other worlds don't suffer eternal thirst raises theological questions

**First Contact:**
When Xuartlek's portals opened, Khashayari citizens entering other worlds experienced water abundance as nearly divine. The psychological impact of seeing rain for the first time, or rivers not guarded by armed soldiers, fundamentally shifted some cultural assumptions. A small but growing philosophical movement questions whether the gods' judgment extends to other worlds--and if not, why?
