## Description

Where three moons dance in impossible chaos above endless seas, and every seventeen years the dead world swings close enough to send the drowned walking home--an archipelago realm where women navigate by sacred starlight and entire civilizations prepare lifetimes for the nights when the barrier between life and death grows thin as morning fog.

## Naming Conventions

Phoenician/Canaanite influences throughout:
* **Tyros**: The star (named for legendary maritime city-state, Greek form of Phoenician Ṣūr = "rock")
* **Shubae**: The world (Phoenician root šub = "return/cycle," reflecting tidal nature)
* **Shacharon**: Inner scorched world (Phoenician/Hebrew שַׁחַר shachar = "dawn")
* **Tanithiel**: Silver moon (Phoenician Tanit = goddess of Carthage + -el = divine suffix)
* **Baelorn**: Golden moon (Phoenician Ba'al = "lord" + orn = "light")
* **Dídhiel**: Violet moon (Phoenician variant + -el = divine suffix)
* **Yammon**: Gas giant prison (Phoenician Yamm/Yam = god of sea and chaos)
* **Molokhon**: Dead world (Phoenician Moloch/Melqart = associated with sacrifice)
* **Meliora**: Northwest continent (Phoenician mlḥ = "sailor" + ora = "coast," "Sailor's Shore")
* **Yamaria**: Eastern continent (Phoenician yam = "sea" + aria = "lion/strong," "Sea-Strong Land")
* **The Three Sisters**: The navigator-goddesses who became moons
* **The Turning**: Biannual tidal catastrophe when all moons align
* **The Drowning**: ~17-18 year event when Molokhon approaches during The Turning

## Full Description

Shubae is a world where water dominates and the ocean's rhythm dictates all life. Vast seas stretch between scattered archipelagos, their shores rising and falling with tides so complex that navigation becomes an art form requiring divine inspiration. The sky displays three moons in constant motion--silver Tanithiel racing along the equator, golden Baelorn arcing over the poles, and violet Dídhiel moving backward against the stars--their chaotic dance a reminder of the Three Sisters who became celestial guardians to save this world from drowning.

The dominant cultures of Shubae are maritime peoples who have learned to read the moons as others read books. The navigator-priesthood, traditionally composed of women following the example of the legendary Three Sisters, maintains temples in every coastal city where they chart the tides and teach the sacred arts of wayfinding. Though recent centuries have opened the priesthood to all genders, women remain the majority and most respected navigators, their authority rooted in myth and millennia of tradition.

The seas themselves hold secrets deeper than any tide chart. Shubae's oceans contain more portals to Aquara, the Elemental Plane of Water, than almost any other known world--dozens of threshold points where the ocean becomes so pure, so perfectly itself, that reality grows thin. These abyssal gates are known to the navigator-priesthood and closely guarded. Some lie in the deepest trenches where no light reaches. Others swirl in permanent maelstroms that have raged for centuries. The merfolk and athamaru speak of places where one can swim from the Material Plane into Aquara without realizing the transition, where the water grows impossibly clear and time flows like current.

Architecture on Shubae reflects the reality of The Turning, the twice-yearly convergence when all three moons align and catastrophic tides reshape coastlines. Coastal cities are built on massive stone foundations that can withstand flooding, with channels and locks designed to manage the deluge. Island communities maintain evacuation plans passed down through generations, while underwater civilizations of merfolk, athamaru, and other aquatic peoples thrive in the stable depths below the surface chaos.

But the true terror is not The Turning--it is The Drowning.

## Astronomical Features

**Three Chaotic Moons:**

The Three Sisters trace impossibly complex orbits that should not be stable--yet have remained so for recorded history. Their dance creates the most complex tidal patterns in the known Material Plane.

**Tanithiel (The Calm Sister):**
* 12.33-day orbital period
* Prograde equatorial orbit (7.5° inclination)
* Silver appearance, brightest of the three
* Master of trade winds and safe passages
* Navigators invoke her for routine voyages
* "Tanithiel's blessing" = calm seas, fair winds

**Baelorn (The Storm Sister):**
* 14.23-day orbital period
* Polar orbit (91° inclination)
* Golden appearance, associated with weather
* Master of polar navigation and storm reading
* Invoked during dangerous passages
* "Baelorn's eye" = the calm center of storms

**Dídhiel (The Deep Sister):**
* 16.81-day orbital period
* Retrograde orbit (192° inclination--moves backward)
* Violet appearance, associated with mystery
* Master of counter-currents and hidden ways
* Invoked for secret journeys, finding the lost
* "Dídhiel's path" = unconventional routes that succeed

**The Turning (Twice Yearly):**
At the spring and autumn equinoxes, all three moons converge on one side of Shubae:
* **Facing side**: Extreme high tides flood entire coastlines
* **Opposite side**: Extreme low tides expose deadly reefs
* Duration: 3-5 days of dangerous conditions
* Coastal populations evacuate to high ground
* Underwater peoples find conditions turbulent even at depth
* Navigator-priesthood maintains constant watch during approach

**Yammon (The Bound God):**
* Gas giant in outer system, 1800-day orbit
* Six moons named for the chains binding him: Adonel, Baeliel, Dagorel, Eshmarel, Kotharil, Melqoril
* Mythology: Contains the imprisoned form of the primordial chaos deity
* The Three Sisters' sacrifice chained him in eternal orbit
* Visible as a bright point of light, ominous associations
* Some cults secretly worship Yammon, seeking to free him

**Molokhon (The World of the Drowned):**
* Captured rogue planet, highly elliptical orbit
* ~8.73 Shubese year orbital period (~9.6 Earth years)
* Eccentricity 0.45 (swings from far to dangerously close)
* Dark, cold, lifeless surface--a necropolis
* Populated by souls of the drowned who died without proper rites
* Periapsis brings it close enough to influence tides and minds

**The Drowning (~Every 17-18 Shubese Years):**
When Molokhon's closest approach coincides with The Turning:
* The barrier between life and death grows thin
* Souls trapped on Molokhon can cross the void
* The drowned dead return to walk among the living
* Mass hauntings, possessions, undead incursions
* Most feared astronomical event in Shubese culture
* Entire civilization organized around preparation for these events

* **Humans** (30%): Skilled and Versatile heritages. Dominant in Meliora, major traders and sailors.
* **Halflings** (10%): Nomadic heritage. Island-hopping traders, excellent small-boat sailors.
* **Catfolk** (5%): Jungle heritage. Coastal forest communities, fishing specialists.
* **Tengu** (5%): Stormtossed and Wavediver heritages. Storm-readers, cliff-dwelling communities.
* **Leshy** (5%): Lotus and Seaweed heritages. Coastal guardians, tide pool tenders.

**Aquatic Peoples:**
* **Merfolk** (15%): Abyssal, Carcharodon, Pelagic, Reef, and Sailfish heritages. Yamaria dominant culture, underwater cities.
* **Athamaru/Tritons** (10%): Coral, Hopeful, Kaleidoscopic, Quilled heritages. Southern archipelago ancestral heartland, deep-water specialists.
* **Undine** (5%): Versatile. Water-touched humans, bridge between surface and sea.

**Other Peoples:**
* **Lizardfolk** (5%): Bakuwa and Wetlander heritages. Coastal swamp communities.
* **Kobold** (3%): Tunnelflood heritage. Coastal cave systems, salvage specialists.
* **Others** (7%): Orc (Rainfall), Centaur (Ponygait), Kholo (Sweetbreath), Ratfolk (Longsnout), Tripkee (Riverside), Wayang (Sailor)

**The Navigator-Priesthood:**

Central religious and civic institution of Shubese culture:

**Structure:**
* Temple-complexes in every coastal city
* Traditionally women-only, now open to all genders
* Women remain majority (~80%) and most respected
* Authority rooted in the Three Sisters mythology
* Combines religious, navigational, and political roles

**Responsibilities:**
* Charting tides and predicting The Turning
* Teaching sacred arts of wayfinding
* Maintaining astronomical records
* Performing rites for sailors and the dead
* Preparing communities for The Drowning
* Guarding knowledge of Aquara portals

**Training:**
* Begins in childhood for those showing aptitude
* Full priesthood requires decades of study
* Specializations: tide-reading, star-navigation, death rites, portal knowledge
* Highest rank: "Sister of the Three" (regardless of gender)

**Magic Level:** Moderate
* Strong nautical and divination traditions
* Water magic common among all peoples
* Death rites essential for preventing drowned souls
* Navigator-priesthood maintains arcane traditions
* Some primal magic in isolated island communities
* Necromancy strictly forbidden (creates more drowned)

**Technology Level:** Age of Exploration
* Advanced navigation instruments
* Sophisticated shipbuilding traditions
* Flood architecture engineering
* Aquaculture and fishing technology
* Limited metallurgy (few mineral resources)
* Glass and coral crafts highly developed

**Death Rites:**

Perhaps the most critical cultural practice:

**The Problem:**
Anyone who dies at sea without proper rites risks their soul being drawn to Molokhon instead of passing on. During The Drowning, these trapped souls return--hostile, confused, seeking completion.

**The Solution:**
* All sailors carry "soul-anchors"--blessed tokens binding their spirit to shore
* Bodies must be recovered for proper burial when possible
* When bodies cannot be recovered, surrogate rites use personal effects
* Navigator-priestesses perform "calling back" rituals to guide lost souls
* Annual remembrance festivals honor the drowned and reinforce protections

**Cultural Impact:**
* No Shubese person travels by sea without soul-anchor
* Leaving the dead unburied is worst possible crime
* Salvage operations prioritize recovering bodies over cargo
* The Drowning preparations begin years in advance
* Entire economic system accounts for 17-18 year cycle

**Social Structure:**
* City-states in Meliora (human-dominant)
* Kingdom of Dagriarravenia in Yamaria (merfolk-human)
* Tribal confederations in southern archipelagos (athamaru)
* Navigator-priesthood transcends political boundaries
* Maritime law recognized across all cultures

**Religion:**
* Three Sisters worship dominant
* Yammon cults suppressed but persistent
* Ancestor veneration (especially the drowned)
* Aquara as spiritual ideal of water's perfection
* Death as transition, not ending (unless drowned)

**Elemental Plane Connections:**

Shubae contains dozens of natural Aquara portals:

* **Abyssal Gates**: Deepest trenches, no light, pressure beyond surface tolerance
* **Maelstrom Thresholds**: Permanent whirlpools marking transition zones
* **Pure Water Zones**: Areas where ocean becomes "perfectly itself"
* **Transition Experience**: Gradual--water grows impossibly clear, time flows strangely
* **Navigation**: Known to priesthood, locations closely guarded
* **Merfolk Knowledge**: Some can accidentally cross when water is pure enough

**Theme:** Phoenician-Inspired Maritime Fantasy with Celestial Mythology and Undead Horror Elements

## History

**The Age of Chaos (Mythological):**

Before recorded history, Yammon the chaos-god ruled the seas. His storms drowned continents, his currents pulled sailors into the void, his hunger was endless. The Three Sisters--Tanithiel, Baelorn, and Dídhiel--were mortal navigator-priestesses who led a coalition of surface and aquatic peoples against the god's tyranny.

The battle lasted generations. Finally, the Sisters discovered Yammon could not be destroyed, only bound. They sacrificed themselves to become celestial chains, their souls transformed into moons that would orbit Shubae forever, their presence keeping Yammon imprisoned in the outer system.

In his final act of defiance, Yammon hurled Molokhon--a dead world from beyond the stars--at Shubae. The Three Sisters caught it mid-strike, trapping it in eternal orbit. Forever falling, never landing. But Molokhon carried a curse: it would collect the souls of the drowned, and when it swung close, those souls would return.

**The First Drowning (Historical):**

The earliest recorded Drowning occurred approximately 3,000 years ago, devastating unprepared coastal civilizations. Entire cities fell to the returning dead. The catastrophe led to the formalization of the navigator-priesthood and the development of death rites designed to prevent souls from reaching Molokhon.

**The Rise of Maritime Civilization:**

Over millennia, Shubese cultures adapted to their astronomical reality:
* Flood architecture developed and refined
* Death rites became universal cultural practice
* Navigator-priesthood gained religious and political authority
* The 17-18 year Drowning cycle shaped economics, architecture, and society
* Aquatic peoples integrated more fully with surface civilizations

**The Kingdom of Dagriarravenia:**

Approximately 800 years ago, merfolk and human populations in Yamaria unified under a single crown, creating the largest political entity in Shubese history. The Kingdom demonstrates that surface-aquatic cooperation can produce stable, prosperous civilization.

**The Opening of the Priesthood:**

Traditionally, only women could serve as navigator-priestesses, following the example of the Three Sisters. Approximately 200 years ago, a reform movement opened the priesthood to all genders. The change was controversial but ultimately accepted. Women remain the majority and most respected navigators, but the institution now welcomes anyone with aptitude.

**Xuartlek Connection:**

**The Three Districts:**
* **Saluzzo**: Oldest district, established in Meliora, primary trade hub
* **Issoire**: Yamaria district, facilitates surface-aquatic commerce
* **Amisra**: Southern archipelago district, newest, positioned for athamaru trade

**Why Xuartlek Came:**
Shubae's complex tidal patterns and Aquara portal concentration made it astronomically and magically fascinating. Xuartlek scholars spent decades studying the Three Sisters' impossible orbital mechanics and the natural planar connections in the deep ocean.

**Trade:**
* **Exports**: Advanced navigation techniques, ship designs, tidal charts, seafood, pearls, coral crafts, maritime law expertise, death rite knowledge
* **Imports**: Metal goods, agricultural products, magical components, news from worlds without oceans
* **Unique Exchange**: Navigator-priesthood training available to multiverse scholars

**The Drowning Preparation:**
Xuartlek districts participate in Drowning preparations alongside local populations. Portal connections provide evacuation routes during the worst events, and Xuartlek scholars have contributed to understanding (though not preventing) the phenomenon.
