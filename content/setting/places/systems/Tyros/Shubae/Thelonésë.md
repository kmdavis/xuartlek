Thelonésë is a port town on an island in the western reaches of the Principality of Zolnuth, on the world of Shubae in the Tyros system. Unlike its sister port Sielmoro to the southeast, Thelonésë maintains at least a veneer of proper governance--constables patrol the streets, courts convene, and the Principality's law carries weight here. It serves as a waypoint for ships traveling between the outer islands and the Zolnuthian mainland.

## Overview

The town occupies a natural harbor on the island's northwest coast. A fortified government complex--part garrison, part courthouse, part jail--overlooks the harbor district, a visible reminder that law has teeth here. The streets are busier than Sielmoro's, with a mix of sailors, merchants, and locals going about legitimate business alongside the inevitable shadow trade that any port attracts.

**Population**: ~3,000 permanent residents, plus transient sailors and merchants

**Government**: Local constables maintain order under Zolnuthian authority. A circuit judge travels between the outer island settlements to handle serious crimes--petty matters are dealt with on the spot, but anything requiring a proper trial waits for the judge's arrival.

**Economy**: Trade, shipping, and services for vessels passing through. More legitimate commerce than Sielmoro, though the same "salvage" trade flows through both ports. The island's interior supports timber operations and mixed agriculture, with goods flowing through Thelonésë for export.

## The Island

Unlike Sielmoro's uninhabited island, Thelonésë's island supports a significant inland population. Forested hills provide tropical hardwoods for shipbuilding, while cleared lowlands produce provisions for the port and passing ships.

**Inland Population**: ~6,000 across several villages and logging camps

**Economy**: Timber (tropical hardwoods), mixed agriculture (fruit, provisions), and rope fiber production

**Political Structure**: Two or three elven noble families hold land grants from the Principality, managing the timber operations and agricultural estates. The port handles trade and Zolnuthian administration, while the inland communities maintain their own rhythms under the lords' authority. Relations between port and interior are generally cordial, if sometimes tense over prices and labor conditions.

**Demographics**: The inland population skews toward Catfolk (jungle heritage), Humans, and Lizardfolk, while the port is more cosmopolitan with Elves in positions of authority.

## Key Locations

### The Government Complex
A fortified stone building that serves as the seat of Zolnuthian authority on the island. Houses the constabulary, a small jail, and the courtroom where the circuit judge holds session. More imposing than anything in Sielmoro--a deliberate statement about who holds power here.

### The Harbor
The town's lifeblood. Ships from across the Principality and beyond dock here, loading and unloading cargo under the watchful eye of the port master's office.

### The Port Master's Office
Handles docking fees, cargo manifests, and the endless paperwork of maritime commerce. A good place to find work or information about ships passing through.

### The Bazaar
An open-air market where merchants hawk everything from ship supplies to exotic goods. More regulated than Sielmoro's market--the constables keep an eye out for obviously stolen merchandise, though plenty still changes hands.

### The Mariner's Rest
The town's main tavern and inn, situated near the harbor. Respectable enough for merchants but rough enough for sailors--the sweet spot that keeps both crowds happy. News travels fast through the common room, and the bulletin board near the entrance posts job opportunities for those willing to work.

### The Spring Tide
A "private social club" in the northeast quarter of town, catering to those with coin and discretion. Membership costs 5 silver and grants access to card games, dice, and other entertainments that the constabulary officially doesn't see. The sort of place where business gets done over drinks, away from prying eyes.

## The Rural Lords

Three elven noble families hold land grants on the island, managing the timber operations and agricultural estates that feed Thelonésë's exports.

**Lord Aldren Maevathar**: The eldest and wealthiest of the island's lords. Controls most of the timber operations from an estate in the eastern hills. Father of Isindrel. Outwardly respectable, strict about accounts and schedules.

**Lady Seraphine Olythos**: A widow who inherited her husband's agricultural plantation. Runs it competently, treats workers fairly, and is widely respected in both port and interior. Potential ally for those seeking to navigate island politics.

**Lord Caelen Thassaril**: The youngest lord, more interested in merchant ventures than land management. Frequently in the capital, rarely on the island. His estate is managed by stewards who do the actual work.

## Law & Order

Unlike Sielmoro's laissez-faire approach, Thelonésë enforces the Principality's laws:

**Constables**: Pairs of constables patrol the streets wearing leather armor and carrying short swords. They're not soldiers, but they're not pushovers either. They respond to disturbances, investigate crimes, and maintain the peace.

**The Circuit Judge**: Serious crimes wait for the circuit judge, who travels a regular route between the outer island settlements. Minor matters--bar fights, petty theft--are handled on the spot. Anything that might warrant hanging requires the judge's presence. The wait can range from days to weeks depending on the circuit schedule.

**Self-Defense**: The law recognizes self-defense, but killing someone in a public fight will still draw constable attention. Expect questions, witness statements, and potentially being held until the judge can review the case.

## Plot Hooks

### Stuck in Port
Someone in the party has attracted constable attention. They're not under arrest, but they can't leave town until the circuit judge reviews their case. Time to find work and keep a low profile--or find a way to resolve the matter before the judge arrives.

### The Judge's Circuit
The circuit judge is due any day now. Everyone with pending cases--legitimate or otherwise--is suddenly very interested in who might testify and what stories might be told. Information, alibis, and witnesses are all commodities.

### Passage West
Ships heading to the outer islands or beyond often stop in Thelonésë to resupply and hire crew. It's a good place to book passage or find work aboard a vessel, though the destinations get more dangerous the further west you sail.

### Labor Troubles
Tensions between the timber workers and the rural lords are running high. The guild is pushing for better conditions; the lords are pushing back. Both sides might find use for capable outsiders willing to do work that locals can't--or won't.

### The Spring Tide's Back Room
Yannick Corwen's social club attracts the wealthy and the desperate in equal measure. Some members have lost more than they can afford. Some have won more than they should. The games at The Spring Tide are never just about cards.
