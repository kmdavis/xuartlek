# Sielmoro

![[Sielmoro settlement.webp]]

A barely-disguised haven for privateers in the Principality of Zolnuth--pirates with just enough legal cover to avoid the noose. Think Mos Eisley with better weather.

Sielmoro is a bustling port town on an island in the Principality of Zolnuth, on the world of Shubae in the Tyros system. Officially a Zolnuthian trading outpost, it operates as a barely-disguised haven for privateers. The Principality finds this arrangement convenient: plausible deniability and a cut of the profits. You'll never find a more wretched hive of scum and villainy, but at least there's good seafood.

The town sprawls along a curved harbor, with wooden docks extending into turquoise waters. Palm trees dot the streets between timber-framed buildings with terracotta roofs. The architecture reflects the town's dual nature--respectable merchant facades hiding smugglers' cellars and escape routes. Notably absent are walls or fortifications--Sielmoro relies on its remote location and the Principality's fleet for protection. The surrounding jungle creeps close to town's edge, lending the place a Caribbean feel.

**Population**: ~2,000 permanent residents, plus 500-1,000 transients (sailors, privateers, merchants)

**Government**: Governor Alandrin Thesalor serves at the Principality's pleasure, balancing official duties with unofficial arrangements.

**Economy**: Ship repairs, provisioning, "salvage" sales, and services for crews flush with prize money. The Siren's Call charges 1 copper per mug of ale, 2 silver per keg--reasonable prices that keep the sailors happy and the coin flowing.

## Key Locations

![[Sielmoro npc-locations.webp]]

### The Governor's Residence
The largest building in town, overlooking the harbor from a hill. Alandrin Thesalor conducts official business here--and unofficial business in the wine cellar.

### The Siren's Call
The town's most reputable tavern and inn, run by "Old Salt" Merek. Neutral ground where deals are struck and grudges are (usually) left at the door. The ale-basted reef claws and grilled snapper are legendary around these parts. A bulletin board near the entrance posts job opportunities--legitimate and otherwise.

### The Docks
Three main piers handle everything from fishing boats to privateering vessels. Portmaster Galrin Mercer keeps the chaos organized--for a fee. Docking runs about 1 gold per day per ship, though premium berths closer to the taverns may cost more.

### The Market
An open-air bazaar where "salvaged" goods find new owners. No questions asked, but Hulda Bramwise's general store is the safest place to buy supplies.

Governor Thesalor monitors and selectively endorses piracy masquerading as privateering operations. Openly respectable, he's skilled at balancing the politics of Zolnuth, employing plausible deniability at every turn.

## Plot Hooks

### The Privateering Commission
Governor Thesalor offers letters of marque to capable crews. The work is lucrative but politically complicated--and the line between privateer and pirate is thinner than the parchment the commission is written on. Approved targets include the Arinians (an enemy from the thousand-year war) and pirates who've overstepped by raiding Zolnuthian shipping. The Republic of Shuamë and Viseleyn are strictly off-limits--attack their vessels and the letter of marque becomes a death warrant.

### The Rival Captains
Two privateering captains are feuding, and the town is taking sides. The party might be hired by one, caught in the middle, or see an opportunity in the chaos.

### The Principality's Interest
An inspector from Zolnuth is coming to audit the colony's books. Suddenly, everyone in town needs favors--hiding cargo, creating alibis, or making sure certain people aren't around when the inspector arrives.

### The Ghost Ship
Sailors speak of a vessel that appears in fog, crewed by the dead. Dreeva Windsong has a chart showing where it was last seen. Is it a haunt, a rival's trick, or something worse?

### The Commodore's Shadow
Commodore Marcellus "Three Sheets" Thorne's influence extends even here--a successful pirate captain with several ships under his command, often depicted with a brace of pistols at his belt. His enforcers, like Korren "Red Sash" (a pit fighter who favors a whip), do his bidding across the islands. Thorne marks his enemies with singed playing cards--a calling card that means you've been noticed. His wanted posters offer 500 gold bounties for those who've crossed him, citing "crimes against the Commodore." Someone in Sielmoro is feeding him information about ships and cargo. Finding the spy could earn powerful friends--or powerful enemies.

### The Ancient Ruins of Zorg
Deep in the island's interior--north-northeast over the hills, in a valley ringed by steep cliffs and fed by waterfalls--lie ancient ruins that draw treasure hunters and their crews. The jungle swallows expeditions whole. Captain Jonas led eleven crew members into those ruins seeking powerful artifacts; none returned. Quartermaster Dominic waits nervously at the docks with the remaining crew, hoping someone will bring back their captain. He's willing to trade ship repairs for news--or rescue.
