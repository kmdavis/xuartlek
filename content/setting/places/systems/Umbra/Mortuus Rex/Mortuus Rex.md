## Description

Where darkness won a thousand years ago and the last bastion of light clings to precarious survival--a world of perpetual twilight beneath a blood-red moon and the shattered remains of its sister, where the armies of the dead hold everything beyond the final walls and hope is measured in generations, not lifetimes.

## Naming Conventions

Latin and Gothic influences reflecting grim-dark themes:
* **Umbra**: The star (Latin = "shadow")
* **Mortuus Rex**: The world (Latin = "Dead King" or "King of the Dead")
* **Sanguis**: The Invader (Latin = "blood")--rogue world that arrived 1000 years ago, prison and throne of the death god
* **Fractum**: The Shattered (Latin = "broken")--debris field, once home to a god of light before Sanguis destroyed it
* **The Veil**: Meteor shower zone from Fractum's debris
* **The Bastion**: Last remaining free territory, a peninsula
* **The Isthmus**: Narrow land bridge connecting the Bastion to the mainland--the frontline
* **The Darklands**: Everything beyond the Isthmus, controlled by undead
* **Nuzairzad**: Xuartlek district (Tortlian standard naming)

## Full Description

Mortuus Rex is a world under siege. A thousand years ago, darkness arrived from the void--uninvited, unprovoked, absolute. Not completely victorious--not yet--but enough that everything outside one fortified peninsula belongs to the dead. The last Bastion endures through desperate defense along a narrow isthmus, sustained by the wounded presence of a fallen god, and the grim knowledge that every generation the line holds is a victory against extinction.

The sky tells the story of the Cataclysm. Sanguis dominates the heavens--not a moon born of this world, but a rogue planet that arrived a millennium ago, captured into orbit by forces beyond natural astronomy. Its deep crimson surface is the prison and throne of the death god who rides within. Opposite it, always setting as Sanguis rises, lies Fractum's debris field--the shattered remains of what was once home to a god of light, destroyed in Sanguis's first and only attack. The two are locked in perfect opposition, victor and victim, darkness and the memory of what darkness killed.

Within the Bastion peninsula, life continues with desperate normalcy. Cities function, markets operate, children are born and taught and married. But everyone serves in the defense in some capacity--there are no exemptions, no pure civilians. The young learn to hold weapons before they learn to read. The old maintain wards until their last breath. Religion centers on the wounded god who fell to save them, whose presence prevents the death god's necromancy from directly affecting Bastion soil, but who cannot heal while sustaining the wards that keep the people alive.

The Isthmus is the frontline--a narrow land bridge connecting the Bastion peninsula to the mainland. Here, the walls are thickest, the wards strongest, the defenders most numerous. Beyond lies the Darklands, stretching to every horizon. Undead armies patrol territories that were once kingdoms, now hunting grounds. Necromancers rule from the bones of fallen cities. Corrupted beings that were once mortal serve a master that was never of this world. Occasionally, the dead test the Isthmus defenses--probing attacks, siege attempts, diplomatic missions from liches seeking surrender. The defenders have held for a thousand years. They intend to hold for a thousand more.

## Physical Characteristics

**Orbital Period:** Unknown (astronomical observation limited by constant twilight)
**Rotation Period:** Standard day/night cycle, but perpetual twilight conditions
**Diameter:** Earth-sized
**Gravity:** Standard
**Atmosphere:** Breathable but thick with dust from Fractum debris

**Climate:**
* Perpetual twilight from Sanguis's red light and Fractum's debris blocking direct sunlight
* The Veil creates constant meteor showers in certain latitudes
* Temperatures cooler than pre-Cataclysm records suggest
* Agricultural zones require magical enhancement to produce sufficient food

## Geography

**The Bastion Peninsula:**
* Last free territory, a large peninsula approximately the size of Iberia
* Connected to mainland only by the Isthmus--a narrow land bridge
* Contains multiple cities, agricultural districts, and military installations
* Population concentrated but sustainable
* Every inch defended, every resource allocated
* The wounded light god fell here, and its presence sanctifies the land

**The Isthmus (The Frontline):**
* Narrow land bridge connecting the Bastion to the mainland
* Approximately 20 miles wide at narrowest point
* Massive walls, layered fortifications, overlapping ward systems
* Where all major battles are fought
* Controlled terrain--undead cannot simply flow around the defenses
* Has never been breached in a thousand years

**The Darklands:**
* Everything beyond the Isthmus
* Former kingdoms, now undead-controlled territories
* Ruins of pre-Cataclysm civilization scattered throughout
* Necromancer strongholds in former capitals
* Hunting grounds where the living are prey
* The death god's necromancy works freely here

**The Veil Zone:**
* Band of latitude where Fractum's debris falls most frequently
* Meteor showers daily, sometimes hourly
* Creates natural hazard--difficult for large armies to cross
* The Bastion positioned partially within this zone for defensive advantage
* Some meteor fragments contain residual light-god essence--valuable for wards

**Beyond the Darklands:**
* Rumors of other survivor enclaves, never confirmed
* Legends of lands where the dead haven't reached
* Exploration parties occasionally sent; few return
* Maps from before the Cataclysm show continents now entirely unknown

## Astronomical Features

**Sanguis (The Blood Moon):**
* Large moon with iron-oxide rich surface
* Deep red coloration visible day and night
* Provides primary illumination during "night" hours
* Associated with undead magic--power spikes during Sanguis-zenith
* Approximately 28-day orbital cycle
* Cultural significance: Symbol of the darkness that came

**Fractum (The Shattered Moon):**
* Debris field where second moon once orbited
* Broke apart approximately 1000 years ago (coinciding with the Cataclysm)
* Creates "The Veil"--constant meteor showers across equatorial band
* Debris slowly spiraling inward; in geological time, will all fall
* Some fragments large enough to be visible as distinct objects
* Cultural significance: Symbol of what was lost

**Oppositional Orbit:**
* Sanguis and Fractum always opposite in sky
* When Sanguis rises, Fractum sets
* When Fractum is overhead, Sanguis is below horizon
* Creates perpetual duality--darkness is always somewhere
* This opposition is not natural

**The Darkening:**
* Brief period each day when both Sanguis and Fractum are near horizon
* Closest thing to true night Mortuus Rex experiences
* Undead are strongest during The Darkening
* Attacks historically concentrated during these hours
* Wards reinforced, defenders doubled during this window

## Culture

**Species (Pathfinder 2E):**
* **Humans** (40%): Majority population, all heritages represented. Every human serves in defense.
* **Aasimar** (15%): Celestial-touched, considered blessed. Often become paladins or priests. Higher survival rates against undead.
* **Elves** (High Elf heritage, 10%): Long-lived, many remember the early centuries of siege. Institutional memory keepers.
* **Half-Elves** (10%): Bridge communities, often serve as officers and diplomats.
* **Half-Orcs** (10%): Valued for combat prowess, integrated fully into Bastion society.
* **Orcs** (10%): Warrior traditions adapted to wall defense. Some clans maintain pre-Cataclysm customs.
* **Tieflings** (5%): Fiend-touched, face suspicion but have proven loyalty over centuries. Some have unique resistances to necrotic energy.

**Magic Level:** High
* Magic essential for survival--wards, healing, food enhancement, weapon enchantment
* Every citizen receives basic magical training
* Necromancy strictly forbidden within Bastion (capital offense)
* Divine magic especially valued for anti-undead properties
* Arcane research focused on defensive applications

**Technology Level:** Renaissance (Stagnant)
* Pre-Cataclysm knowledge preserved but little new development
* Resources directed to defense rather than innovation
* Some advanced metallurgy for weapons and armor
* Alchemical traditions strong (potions of healing, holy water production)
* Printing press enables knowledge preservation and dissemination

**Social Structure:**
* Military hierarchy integrated with civilian governance
* Every citizen has defensive role regardless of primary profession
* Children trained from age 8 in basic combat
* Elderly maintain wards and teach until death
* No exemptions--even the disabled contribute

**Religion:**
* Pantheon worship focused on gods of light, protection, war
* Hope theology: Eventual victory is prophesied (but timeline unclear)
* Ancestor veneration carefully separated from necromancy
* Priests double as combat medics and morale officers
* Some heretical cults secretly worship the darkness (hunted aggressively)

**Culture of Siege:**
* "Normal" life maintained as psychological defense
* Arts, music, theater continue--morale is strategic resource
* Marriage and family encouraged--population must be maintained
* Education emphasizes both practical skills and cultural heritage
* "We endure" is common saying--survival is victory

**Theme:** Grim-Dark Fantasy / Siege Survival

## History

**The World Before:**
Records speak of a world much like others--multiple kingdoms, political conflicts, ordinary problems. Magic was common but not dominant. The two moons--Sanguis and its bright silver sister--lit the nights. Civilization flourished.

**The Cataclysm (1000 years ago):**
The exact cause remains debated:
* Some records suggest a massive necromantic ritual gone wrong
* Others point to an invasion from another plane
* Still others claim a god died, and death itself grew stronger
* The silver moon shattered, becoming Fractum
* The dead rose across the entire world simultaneously
* Kingdoms fell in months, then weeks, then days

**The Retreat:**
Survivors fled toward defensible positions. Most were overrun. The ancestors of the Bastion reached a fortified plateau region and held--barely. The first walls were built from desperation, then rebuilt from determination.

**The Long Siege (1000 years):**
* First Century: Constant attacks, walls nearly breached multiple times
* Second Century: Equilibrium established, walls strengthened
* Centuries 3-5: Expansion attempts all failed, consolidation period
* Centuries 6-8: Cultural renaissance within siege conditions
* Centuries 9-10: Current era, stable but stagnant

**Xuartlek Connection:**
* **Nuzairzad District**: Founded 465 IR, portal completed 475 IR. Hostile
  undead interference made it the slowest primary portal in imperial history,
  ten years against the usual five. See [[timeline]].
* Xuartlek's arrival provided the first external contact since the Cataclysm
* Proof that life existed beyond Mortuus Rex was psychologically transformative
* Trade: Weapons, holy items, healing supplies imported; few exports (what do the besieged have to offer?)
* Strategic concern: Portal provides evacuation route if Bastion falls
* Connected to Strafmack (Ciliren) and Myrrhina (Sulveig)

**The Question:**
Why hasn't the darkness finished conquering Mortuus Rex? The undead armies are vast enough. The answer varies by theory:
* The Bastion's wards are uniquely powerful
* The undead are patient and prefer slow corruption
* Something within the Bastion is valuable and must be taken intact
* The siege itself serves some purpose
* The darkness isn't unified--internal conflicts prevent coordinated assault
