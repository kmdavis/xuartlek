THE SHENZHOU SYSTEM (神州系统 Shénzhōu Xìtǒng)

NAMING CONVENTIONS:
- Star: Shenzhou (神州 Shénzhōu - "Divine Land" or "Sacred Continent")
Traditional name for China; here represents the whole system as sacred space
- Qigang (祁岗 Qígāng): Possibly "prayer/auspicious + hill/ridge"
The habitable world where honor matters more than life
- Moons (六月 Liù Yuè - "Six Moons" or "Dragon's Scales"):
* Mù Yuè (木月 Wood Moon) - 400 km, greenish, organic-rich
* Shuǐ Yuè (水月 Water Moon) - 550 km, bluish-white, water-ice rich
* Tǔ Yuè (土月 Earth Moon) - 600 km, brownish, rocky mantle
* Jīn Yuè (金月 Metal Moon) - 1200 km, silvery, iron/nickel core (brightest)
* Huǒ Yuè (火月 Fire Moon) - 500 km, reddish, iron-oxide crust
* Qì Yuè (气月 Air Moon) - 300 km, faint, volatile/gas-ice (dimmest)

THE CELESTIAL DRAGON - TIAN LONG (天龙):
The night sky of Qigang is dominated by two spectacular features that ancient
mythology explains as a single divine entity: Tian Long, the Celestial Dragon
whose body wraps around the world in eternal watchfulness.

THE DRAGON'S BODY (Ring System):
Six distinct ring bands circle Qigang, each associated with one of the six elements
of Qigangese cosmology (different from Earth's traditional five). The rings appear
different throughout the year based on viewing angle and solar illumination, creating
a 6-year cycle where each element is "dominant" for one year:
- Wood Ring (木环 Mù Huán): Green-tinted ice particles, innermost
- Fire Ring (火环 Huǒ Huán): Red dust and iron oxide particles
- Earth Ring (土环 Tǔ Huán): Brown/tan rocky particles
- Metal Ring (金环 Jīn Huán): Bright metallic particles (most reflective)
- Water Ring (水环 Shuǐ Huán): Blue water-ice crystals
- Air Ring (气环 Qì Huán): Faint gossamer particles (least visible, outermost)

THE DRAGON'S SCALES (The Six Moons):
Approximately 20,000-30,000 years ago, a large moon passed within Qigang's Roche
limit and was torn apart by tidal forces. This catastrophic event, known in mythology
as "The Shattering" (破碎 Pòsuì), is said to be when the celestial dragon was slain
by ancient heroes, its body fragmenting into the six moons and the ring system.

After millennia of gravitational settling, six major fragments stabilized into a
remarkable co-orbital configuration. All six moons share the same orbital period
(~30 Earth days / ~29 Qigang days) but are clustered within a ~110° arc rather than
evenly spaced. This creates dramatic visual effects:
- "Full Dragon" (满龙 Mǎn Lóng): When entire cluster visible opposite the sun
- "Dragon Rising" (龙升 Lóng Shēng): Sequential appearance of moons as cluster rises
- "Dragon's Spine" (龙脊 Lóng Jǐ): The visible arc connecting all six moons
- "The Dark Time" (暗时 Àn Shí): ~2/3 of each month when no moons are visible

MOON ARRANGEMENT (Relative to Metal Moon at Center):
The moons are NOT evenly spaced but clustered irregularly:

Leading edge:
- Wood Moon (木月): -55° ahead of Metal Moon
* ~400 km diameter, organic-rich mantle fragment
* Greenish tint from carbonaceous material
* Associated with growth, flexibility, spring

- Water Moon (水月): -25° ahead of Metal Moon
* ~550 km diameter, water-ice rich fragment
* Bluish-white coloration
* Associated with flow, adaptability, winter
* Swapped position with Earth for physical realism (water-ice can be denser)

Center cluster (tightly grouped):
- Earth Moon (土月): -8° ahead of Metal Moon
* ~600 km diameter, rocky mantle fragment
* Brownish coloration
* Associated with stability, nourishment, late summer

- Metal Moon (金月): 0° reference point (CENTER)
* ~1200 km diameter - the original moon's iron/nickel core
* Silvery, highly reflective (brightest by far, ~2× all others combined)
* Associated with strength, harvest, autumn
* Dominates the night sky when visible

- Fire Moon (火月): +12° behind Metal Moon
* ~500 km diameter, iron-oxide rich crust fragment
* Reddish appearance
* Associated with energy, transformation, summer

Trailing edge:
- Air Moon (气月): +55° behind Metal Moon
* ~300 km diameter, volatile/gas-ice fragment
* Faint, gossamer appearance (dimmest)
* Associated with freedom, communication, early spring

CULTURAL SIGNIFICANCE:
The irregular spacing creates complex astrological patterns that Qigangese scholars
have studied for millennia. The moons drift slowly over centuries, requiring constant
astronomical updates--a fact that drives much of Qigang's mathematical and astronomical
development. Ancient ruins contain pre-Shattering records describing a different sky,
proving the ancient texts about "when the sky was broken" were literal history, not myth.

THE CALENDAR:
The Qigangese calendar reflects this cosmic harmony:
- 432-day year (72 × 6 = 12 × 36) - harmonically perfect
- 12 months of 36 days (zodiac animals with seasonal descriptors)
- 6-day weeks (elemental cycle matching the rings and moons)
- 72-year grand cycle (12 zodiac × 6 elements)
- Leap system: +1 day every 6 years EXCEPT year 72 (perfect completion)

SYSTEM ARCHITECTURE:

STAR: Shénzhōu (神州星 Divine Land Star) - K0V Orange Dwarf
- Mass: 0.88 M☉
- Luminosity: 0.63 L☉
- Temperature: ~5,250 K (vs Sun's 5,778 K)
- Age: ~6 billion years
- Cooler and longer-lived than the Sun, explaining ring system persistence

FORMATION HISTORY:
~4 billion years ago: System formation from protoplanetary disk
~2 billion years ago: Tiānwáng's migration inward destabilized outer system
~100 million years ago: Ancient impact created Qigang's initial ring system
- Possibly from a large asteroid or small moon passing within Roche limit
- Rings have persisted due to K0V star's lower radiation pressure
~20,000 years ago: The Shattering - Qigang's binary moon system disrupted
- Likely caused by 3-body resonance with Tiānwáng
- Primary moon (Yáng Yuè) broke up within Roche limit
- Secondary moon (Yīn Yuè) also disrupted by tidal forces
- Created 6 major fragments (current moons)
- Added fresh material to existing rings, creating distinct new bands
- Meteor bombardment lasted ~1,000 years
~15,000 years ago: New ring material settled into current 6-band configuration
~5,000 years ago: Qigangese civilization begins recording astronomical events
~3,800 years ago: Current calendar system established

INNER SYSTEM:
1. Huǒxīng (火星 Fire Star) - Hot rocky world, 0.52 AU
- Mercury-analog, too hot for life
- Named for its reddish appearance at dawn/dusk

HABITABLE ZONE:
2. Qigang (祁岗) - Ringed habitable world, 1.072 AU
- 432-day year (perfect harmonic)
- 28-hour day (1.167 Earth days)
- Six co-orbital moons (remnants of binary system)
- Six-band ring system
- Rings lie in plane of Qigang's orbit (0° inclination to ecliptic)
- This creates spectacular seasonal variations as viewing angle changes

OUTER SYSTEM:
3. Suìshí Dài (碎石带 Broken Stone Belt) - Asteroid belt, ~3 AU
- Scattered rocky debris between inner and outer systems
- Possibly remnants from failed planet formation
- Provides minor resources but poses navigation hazards

4. Tiānwáng (天王 Sky King) - Ice giant, 5.8 AU
- Uranus-analog, bluish-green color
- Named for being the first "outer planet" discovered
- Its past migration inward likely triggered the binary moon instability
- Qigangese astronomers cannot detect this ancient migration
- Currently in stable orbit

5. Yōuwáng (幽王 Dark King) - Ice giant, 9.2 AU
- Neptune-analog, deep blue color
- Named for its dimness and difficulty to observe
- Last major planet in the system

BINARY MOON BACKSTORY (The Shattering):
Before 20,000 years ago, Qigang had two large moons in a stable binary configuration:
- Yáng Yuè (阳月 Yang Moon) - 2,400 km diameter, the "bright" moon
- Yīn Yuè (阴月 Yin Moon) - 2,000 km diameter, the "dark" moon

These moons orbited in a yin-yang dance, each eternally chasing the other. Ancient
texts describe them as lovers separated by fate, forever reaching but never touching.

~20,000 years ago, a gravitational resonance with Tiānwáng gradually destabilized
their orbits over centuries. The binary system's barycenter drifted closer to Qigang.
When Yáng Yuè crossed within Qigang's Roche limit (~2.5 planetary radii), tidal
forces tore it apart. The disruption then destabilized Yīn Yuè, which also broke up.

The six current moons are the major fragments:
- Jīn Yuè (Metal Moon, 1200 km) - Yáng Yuè's iron/nickel core (brightest)
- Tǔ Yuè (Earth Moon, 600 km) - Yáng Yuè's rocky mantle
- Huǒ Yuè (Fire Moon, 500 km) - Yáng Yuè's oxidized crust
- Shuǐ Yuè (Water Moon, 550 km) - Yīn Yuè's ice-rich mantle
- Mù Yuè (Wood Moon, 400 km) - Yīn Yuè's carbonaceous mantle
- Qì Yuè (Air Moon, 300 km) - Yīn Yuè's volatile-rich outer layer

The mythology interprets both events as one: Tian Long (the Celestial Dragon) was
slain by ancient heroes, its body shattering into scales (moons) and bones (rings).
In reality, the ancient rings predate the Shattering by ~100 million years, but the
Shattering added fresh material that created the current spectacular 6-band structure.
Modern Qigangese astronomers understand both the ancient rings and recent Shattering,
but the mythology conflates them into a single dramatic event.
