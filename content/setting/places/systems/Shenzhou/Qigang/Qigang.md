## Description

Where six elemental rings shimmer in the heavens and six moons dance together in celestial harmony, ancient kingdoms measure honor in blood and time in cosmic cycles--a world where the sky itself tells the story of catastrophe transformed into beauty, and where astronomers proved that mythology was astronomy all along.

## Naming Conventions

Chinese/Oriental influences throughout:
* **Shenzhou**: The star (神州 = "Divine Land")
* **Qigang**: The world (祁岗 = proper name, suggesting strength/mountain)
* **The Six Moons** (Dragon's Scales): Metal Moon (金月), Wood Moon (木月), Water Moon (水月), Fire Moon (火月), Earth Moon (土月), Air Moon (气月)
* **The Rings**: Six elemental bands corresponding to the six elements
* **Districts**: Daewynn (leonin district), Tarbent (loxodon scholarly district)

## Full Description

Qigang is a world where the weight of tradition balances against the necessity of change, where ancient kingdoms maintain elaborate codes of honor while adapting to the strange influx of visitors from the portals. The sky itself reinforces the cultural obsession with harmony and cycles--the six elemental rings, the six moons in their irregular dance, the six-day weeks, all reflecting the fundamental sixness that permeates Qigangese cosmology.

The world is dominated by vast empires and kingdoms, each claiming legendary descent from heroes who lived during the mythical Age of Breaking when the sky was shattered. These claims are matters of cultural identity rather than documented genealogy--no written records extend back 20,000 years--but the discovery that the myths describe real astronomical events has only strengthened their cultural power. The rings and moons aren't just celestial objects but astrological markers, their positions at one's birth determining auspicious paths and dangerous years.

The ring system dominates the night sky, its six elemental bands shimmering with different colors depending on the season and viewing angle. The rings themselves are ancient--approximately 100 million years old--but approximately 20,000 years ago, a binary moon system broke apart and added fresh material that created the current spectacular six-band configuration visible today. Ancient oral traditions speak of Tian Long, the Celestial Dragon, whose body was shattered by mythical heroes in the Age of Breaking. For millennia, these were considered pure mythology--until approximately 2,000 years ago, when Qigangese astronomers developed sophisticated mathematical models and realized the myths were describing a real astronomical event: the destruction of Yáng Yuè and Yīn Yuè, the binary moons.

These six moons--the Dragon's Scales--share a single orbit but cluster within a 110-degree arc, creating spectacular "Full Dragon" displays when all are visible together, followed by long dark periods when the cluster moves to Qigang's day side. Metal Moon, the original Yáng Yuè's massive iron core, dominates the cluster at nearly twice the brightness of all others combined. Wood Moon and Water Moon follow close behind, their surfaces showing evidence of the ancient cataclysm. Fire Moon glows with residual volcanic activity, its surface still hot from the energy released during the Shattering. Earth Moon, the largest fragment, shows clear impact scarring from the collision. Air Moon trails at the rear, barely visible even on the clearest nights--the smallest fragment, mostly ice and gas.

Two Xuartlek Low City districts connect Qigang to the broader multiverse: Daewynn, where leonin honor-warriors serve as portal guards and mediators, and Tarbent, where loxodon scholars study the astronomical records that proved Qigang's ancient oral traditions were describing real events. These portals arrived only recently in Qigang's long history, yet they've already begun to shift the careful balance--bringing new philosophies, new technologies, and new questions about honor that the ancient codes never contemplated.

Magic exists on Qigang but is subtle, woven into the fabric of daily life rather than explosive and obvious. Elemental resonance with the rings and moons creates six distinct magical traditions, each strongest during its element's dominant year in the six-year cycle. A Fire wizard born during Fire's year, when the Fire ring glows brightest and Fire Moon leads the cluster, is considered blessed by cosmic alignment itself.

## Astronomical Features

**The Six Elemental Rings:**

Ancient ring system (~100 million years old) enhanced by the Shattering 20,000 years ago. Six distinct bands, each associated with an element:

* **Metal Ring** (innermost): Reflective metallic particles, silver-white, brightest during autumn
* **Wood Ring**: Organic compounds and carbon chains, greenish tint, dominant in spring
* **Water Ring**: Ice crystals and frozen volatiles, blue shimmer, prominent in winter
* **Fire Ring**: Iron oxides and silicates, reddish glow, peaks in summer
* **Earth Ring**: Rocky debris and dust, brown-grey, stable year-round
* **Air Ring** (outermost): Gossamer particles and gases, nearly invisible, requires specific viewing angles

**The Six Moons (Dragon's Scales):**

All six moons share a single orbital path but cluster within 110-degree arc:

* **Metal Moon (金月)**: 2,400 km, Yáng Yuè's iron core, dominates cluster at 2× brightness of others
* **Wood Moon (木月)**: 1,800 km, shows impact scarring, greenish surface deposits
* **Water Moon (水月)**: 1,600 km, ice-covered, reflects blue in sunlight
* **Fire Moon (火月)**: 1,400 km, still volcanically active from Shattering energy
* **Earth Moon (土月)**: 2,100 km, largest fragment, clear collision damage
* **Air Moon (气月)**: 800 km, smallest, trailing edge of cluster, barely visible

**Orbital Mechanics:**
* All six complete orbit in 12 Qigangese days (13 Earth days)
* Cluster stays together due to gravitational resonance from the Shattering
* "Full Dragon" when all six visible together (spectacular sight)
* "Dark Dragon" when cluster on day side (long moonless periods)
* Positions at birth determine astrological fortune

**The Myth of Tian Long:**

In the Age of Breaking, Tian Long the Celestial Dragon held the world in terror. His body stretched across the sky, his breath created storms, his movements caused earthquakes. Six heroes--each embodying one of the six elements--united despite their differences to challenge the Dragon. Their battle shattered the heavens, breaking Tian Long's body into pieces that became the moons. His blood painted the rings with elemental colors. His defeat brought the Age of Harmony.

**The Astronomical Truth:**

20,000 years ago, Qigang had a binary moon system: Yáng Yuè (Yang Moon, bright) and Yīn Yuè (Yin Moon, dark). Gravitational perturbations from a passing rogue planet destabilized their orbit, causing them to collide. The impact shattered both moons into six major fragments that settled into a resonant co-orbital configuration. Debris from the collision refreshed and reorganized Qigang's ancient ring system into the current six-band configuration.

Qigangese astronomers proved this approximately 2,000 years ago through mathematical modeling, validating the oral traditions and elevating the Age of Breaking from myth to historical astronomy. The discovery profoundly impacted culture--ancestors literally witnessed and recorded a cosmic catastrophe, passing the knowledge down as mythology until astronomy caught up.

## Culture

**Species (Pathfinder 2E):**
* **Humans** (60%): Dominant culture, most kingdoms human-ruled. Multiple ethnicities based on different ancient kingdoms.
* **Leonin** (15%): Pride-based social structure, often serve as warriors and honor-keepers. Associated with Metal element and autumn season. Northern steppes heartland.
* **Loxodon** (15%): Scholars, historians, monks. Long memories make them keepers of oral traditions and written histories. Associated with Earth element and stability. Mountain monastery-cities.
* **Tabaxi** (10%): Traders, scouts, messengers. Mobility makes them natural intermediaries between kingdoms. Associated with Air element and freedom.

**Honor Culture:**
Complex codes governing behavior between social classes, families, and kingdoms. Losing face can be worse than death. Duels and ritual combat resolve disputes that threaten honor. Different kingdoms have different specific codes, but all share fundamental principles about loyalty, honesty, and proper conduct.

**Ancestor Veneration:**
The dead remain part of society through ritual and remembrance. Genealogical records are sacred texts. Families maintain shrines to ancestors stretching back generations. Major decisions consult ancestral wisdom through divination. Dishonoring ancestors is among the gravest sins.

**Elemental Philosophy:**
Six elements (not five) are understood as physical constants discovered through magical experimentation. Different cultures would discover this independently because it's objectively true--unlike Western five-element systems which are cultural constructs, Qigangese six-element theory matches observable reality in this world.

Each element has associated:
* Season (Metal=autumn, Wood=spring, Water=winter, Fire=summer, Earth=transitions, Air=all seasons)
* Direction (Metal=west, Wood=east, Water=north, Fire=south, Earth=center, Air=above)
* Virtue (Metal=righteousness, Wood=benevolence, Water=wisdom, Fire=propriety, Earth=faithfulness, Air=freedom)
* Magical tradition (distinct spell schools and techniques)

**Calendar Importance:**
* The 72-year grand cycle (12 moon cycles × 6 elemental years) is culturally supreme
* Living through a complete cycle brings immense prestige
* Major life decisions timed to auspicious phases
* Whole industries exist around calendar consultation and astrological prediction
* Historical events dated by position in grand cycle

**Magic Level:** Low
Magic exists and is real, but subtle and woven into life rather than flashy. Elemental magic tied to cosmic cycles is strongest. A Fire wizard during Fire's year (when Fire ring glows brightest and Fire Moon leads cluster) has significantly more power than during other years. This creates natural rotation of magical traditions' dominance.

**Technology Level:** Medieval (Song Dynasty China equivalent, ~1000-1200 CE)
* **Metallurgy**: Advanced iron and steel working, pattern-welded blades as art form
* **Agriculture**: Sophisticated irrigation, terraced farming, crop rotation aligned with six-year cycle
* **Architecture**: Multi-story pagodas, curved roofs, earthquake-resistant design
* **Printing**: Woodblock printing well-established, movable type experimental
* **Navigation**: Compass in use, detailed star charts (driven by astrological needs)
* **Medicine**: Sophisticated herbal medicine, acupuncture, surgery
* **Weaponry**: Crossbows, early gunpowder weapons (fire lances, primitive bombs)
* **Textiles**: Silk production (closely guarded secret), complex weaving, advanced dyes
* **Ceramics**: Porcelain production (another closely guarded secret)

**Social Structure:**
* Imperial/feudal systems dominant
* Scholarly bureaucracy based on competitive examinations
* Merchant class gaining power but still officially lower status than scholars
* Warriors hold honor but scholars hold administrative power
* Complex etiquette and ritual surrounding all interactions

**Theme:** Oriental Fantasy with Cosmic Harmony
Core aesthetic: Ancient Chinese dynasties meets cosmic cycles and honor codes. Key elements: Rings in the sky, six-fold symmetry, ancestor veneration, honor before survival, ancient texts proven true by astronomy.

## History

**The Age of Breaking (20,000 years ago):**
Binary moon collision witnessed by ancestors. Oral traditions preserved the event as mythology about heroes shattering Tian Long. For 18,000 years, treated as pure myth.

**The Astronomical Revelation (~2,000 years ago):**
Qigangese astronomers developed mathematical models showing the moons' collision was real. The myths were ancient astronomy. This discovery fundamentally validated oral tradition and elevated ancestors to near-divine status--they witnessed cosmic catastrophe and preserved the truth through millennia.

**The Warring Kingdoms Period:**
Long era of fragmentation and conflict. Different interpretations of the Breaking mythology fueled ideological warfare. Eventually resolved through philosophical synthesis recognizing multiple truths in the story.

**The Current Equilibrium:**
Multiple powerful kingdoms and empires coexist in tense balance. Honor codes and ritual combat prevent total war. Trade and intermarriage create complex alliance networks.

**Xuartlek Connection (Recent):**
* **Daewynn District**: Leonin honor-warriors serve as portal guards and cultural mediators. Their warrior traditions mesh well with cross-cultural diplomatic needs.
* **Tarbent District**: Loxodon scholars maintain historical records and astronomical observations. Their libraries contain the mathematical models that proved the Breaking was real.

**Why Xuartlek Came:**
Astronomical curiosity and cultural sophistication. Qigang's proof that mythology could encode real astronomical events fascinated Xuartlek's scholars. Also: advanced metallurgy, porcelain, silk, and the honor-based diplomatic system offered trade opportunities.

**Cultural Impact of Portals:**
First contact with worlds that don't share the six-element system raised theological questions. Does cosmic harmony work differently on other worlds? Are the six elements universal or local? Scholars hotly debate these questions, with different kingdoms taking different positions.

**Trade:**
* **Exports**: Silk, porcelain, steel (pattern-welded blades), astronomical charts, honor-bound mercenaries, scholarly services
* **Imports**: Raw materials, magical items (for study), exotic foods, knowledge of other cosmologies
