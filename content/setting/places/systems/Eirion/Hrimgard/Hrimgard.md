## Description

Where a gas giant fills a quarter of the sky and its gravitational heartbeat keeps a frozen moon alive--a world of melancholy poetry and borrowed warmth, where dwarves forge steel in glacier-carved halls, giants keep the old sagas, and every child knows that spring is not given but earned.

## Naming Conventions

Old Norse influences throughout:
* **Eirion**: The star (Celtic/Norse hybrid, "land of the Eir" or healing)
* **Hrimgard**: The habitable moon (Old Norse hrím = frost/rime + garðr = enclosure, "Frost Enclosure")
* **Ymir**: The gas giant (Norse primordial giant from whose body the world was made)
* **Jotunheim**: Sister moon (Norse "Home of the Giants")
* **Niflheim**: Sister moon (Norse realm of ice and mist, land of the dead)
* **Niðhöggr**: Outer ice giant (Norse serpent that gnaws at Yggdrasil's roots)
* **Surtr**: Inner scorched world (Norse fire giant who burns the world at Ragnarök)
* **Folkvangr**: Temperate inner world (Norse "Field of the People," Freyja's afterlife hall)
* **Steinheimr**: Western continent (Old Norse = "Stone-Home," dwarven ancestral homeland)
* **Varðland**: Northern peninsula (Old Norse = "Guardian-Land," goblin ancestral homeland)
* **Fjallgard**: Eastern continent (Old Norse = "Mountain-Enclosure," giant ancestral homeland)
* **Lausavísa**: Traditional verse form (Old Norse = "loose verse," improvisational poetry)

## Full Description

Hrimgard--the Frost Enclosure--is a moon of the gas giant Ymir, orbiting in the frozen outer reaches of the Eirion System. Ice dominates nearly everything, save for the temperate bands north and south of the Spine of the World, an impassable equatorial mountain range thrust up by tidal forces. Between the polar wastes and the volcanic spine lie two temperate belts where life has found purchase--forests of black pine and frost oak, subsistence farms, and settlements of dwarves, giants, and goblins who have learned to live with the cold as a constant companion.

The sky tells Hrimgard's story. Ymir looms so vast one cannot see all of it at once, providing "Ymir-light" bright enough to read by at midnight. Twice each month comes Eclipse--when Hrimgard passes through Ymir's shadow into absolute darkness. These mark the new month in the Ymirian calendar and are treated with religious significance, a time when the boundary between living and dead grows thin. The other moons, Jotunheim and Niflheim, trace their own patterns: Jotunheim's alignment with Ymir creates Spring Tides that threaten coastal settlements, while Niflheim's 45-day orbit drifts gradually out of sync.

Life here breeds a particular poetry. The people understand impermanence--they watch glaciers advance, see storms bury settlements, know warmth is borrowed, never owned. Their sagas are melancholy and beautiful, their art features ice sculptures that last only until the next thaw. A common lausavísa captures this spirit:

> *Five sheep through winter,*
> *frost-bitten but breathing--*
> *Ymir watches, knowing:*
> *we endure, we earn spring.*

As any Hrimgardian will tell you, with frost in their breath and pride in their eyes: "We are not despite the cold. We are because of it."

## Physical Characteristics

**Orbital Period:** 14 days around Ymir (1 Ymirian month)
**Rotation Period:** 7 days (Hrimgard's "day")
**Diameter:** 12,800 km (Earth-sized)
**Gravity:** Standard
**Atmosphere:** Thick enough to retain heat from tidal processes

**Tidal Heating:**
Ymir's massive gravity flexes Hrimgard's interior every orbit, generating internal heat that allows life to survive despite the distance from Eirion. This same process creates the Spine of the World--volcanic mountains thrust up along the equator where tidal stress is greatest.

**Climate Zones:**
* **Polar Wastes**: Uninhabitable, eternal ice, thin atmosphere
* **Temperate Belts**: Cold but survivable, forests and farms possible, where all civilization exists
* **Spine of the World**: Volcanically active, impassable, divides the two temperate zones

## Geography

**The Spine of the World:**
An impassable equatorial volcanic mountain range thrust up by tidal forces from Ymir's gravitational pull. Constantly active, with lava flows and earthquakes. Acts as natural barrier between northern and southern temperate belts. Some dwarven clans maintain observation posts on the lower slopes, but no permanent settlements exist on the Spine itself.

**Temperate Belts:**
Two habitable bands north and south of the Spine where civilization exists:
* Forests of black pine and frost oak
* Subsistence farming in sheltered valleys
* Fortified settlements built to withstand winter storms
* Coastal fishing communities (when not threatened by Spring Tides)

**Three Continents:**

**Steinheimr ("Stone-Home," West):**
* Largest continent spanning both temperate belts
* Dwarven ancestral homeland
* Mountain clans, glacier-forges, deep mines
* Most industrialized region
* Ice-mead brewing centers

**Varðland ("Guardian-Land," North):**
* Harsh northern peninsula, mostly in northern temperate belt
* Goblin ancestral homeland
* Subsistence farming, hardy livestock
* Watchposts monitoring polar weather
* Reputation for toughest survivors

**Fjallgard ("Mountain-Enclosure," East):**
* Eastern continent plus Spine isthmus connection
* Giant ancestral homeland
* Saga-keeper traditions strongest here
* Patrol routes through temperate belt
* Oldest settlements, longest continuous habitation

**Major Island:**
Large northeastern island between Varðland and Fjallgard, shared territory, unnamed in common parlance but contested in old disputes.

**Cultural Note:**
Each ancestry expanded from their homeland over millennia; continental names persist even after extensive population mixing. A dwarf born in Varðland is still Varðlander by culture, Steinheimr-blooded by ancestry.

## Astronomical Features

**Ymir (Gas Giant Primary):**
* Dominates sky, filling roughly 1/4 of the heavens when overhead
* Orange and cream banded atmosphere, like mead swirling in a horn
* Provides "Ymir-light"--reflected sunlight bright enough to read by at midnight
* Rises and sets every 7 days (Hrimgard's rotation period)
* Cultural significance: The transformed body of the primordial giant

**Eclipse:**
* Occurs twice per 14-day month when Hrimgard passes through Ymir's shadow
* Duration: 4-8 hours of absolute darkness
* Marks the new month in the Ymirian calendar
* Religious significance: Ancestors walk, boundary between living and dead grows thin
* Communities gather for remembrance rituals and saga-telling

**Jotunheim (Sister Moon):**
* Amber-brown surface, 28-day orbit around Ymir
* When aligned with Ymir relative to Hrimgard, creates "Spring Tides"
* Spring Tides threaten coastal settlements with extreme flooding
* Navigators and fishermen track its position constantly
* Mythology: Home of the frost giants who never came to Hrimgard

**Niflheim (Sister Moon):**
* Pale icy surface, 45-day orbit around Ymir
* Drifts gradually out of sync with Hrimgard's calendar
* Named for the Norse underworld
* Associated with death, endings, the outer cold
* Some traditions hold that souls travel to Niflheim after death

**Niðhöggr (Outer Ice Giant):**
* Pale blue-white ice giant in distant orbit (~50 year period)
* Named for the serpent that gnaws at Yggdrasil's roots
* Barely visible to naked eye, studied mainly by scholars
* Mythology: The world-serpent frozen and waiting for the final days
* Astronomers dismiss this as superstition; mystics are less certain

## Culture

**Species (Pathfinder 2E):**
* **Dwarves** (45%): Glacier-forged metalworkers, mountain clans, renowned ice-mead brewers. Strong Dwarf and Forge Dwarf heritages common. Masters of working metal in extreme cold--"glacier-forging" produces uniquely resilient steel. Clan structure remains strong, with forge-rights passed through generations.
* **Giants** (30%): Keepers of sagas, patrol the temperate belts, understand the ice and its dangers. Frost Giant heritage dominant. Oral tradition preservers--giants remember what others forget. Respected as historians and judges. Some clans maintain routes through the Spine's lower slopes.
* **Goblins** (25%): Hardy survivors adapted to cold, subsistence farmers who coax crops from frozen ground. Snow Goblin and Treedweller heritages common. Smaller food requirements make them efficient in lean times. Often underestimated, but no less Hrimgardian than any other ancestry.

**Magic Level:** Moderate
* Tied to ancestors, sagas, and runes
* Divination highly respected (communication with ancestors during Eclipses)
* Necromancy practiced openly (ancestors guide the living)
* Ice magic is practical necessity, not specialty
* Runic tradition: Magic bound into carved objects

**Technology Level:** Medieval
* Subsistence farming in sheltered valleys
* Glacier-forging: Unique metallurgy adapted to extreme cold
* Wool and timber trade between communities
* Innovation focuses on survival technologies
* Shipbuilding for coastal fishing and inter-continental trade

**Cultural Themes:**
* **Norse fantasy with melancholy poetry**: Life is hard, beauty is fleeting, endurance is virtue
* **Ice sculpture as ephemeral art**: Works that melt with seasons, impermanence celebrated
* **Sagas and oral tradition**: Written records freeze and become brittle; memory endures
* **Stoic acceptance**: "We endure, we earn spring"--pride in survival itself
* **Ancestor reverence**: The dead guide the living, especially during Eclipse

**Lausavísa Tradition:**
Improvisational verse form used for everything from political commentary to love declarations. Mastery of lausavísa marks cultural sophistication. Giants are acknowledged masters, but all ancestries practice the form.

**Calendar:**
The Ymirian calendar measures time by Eclipses (monthly) and Ymir's position. The 14-day orbital period serves as the primary month. Seasons track Eirion's distant influence more than Ymir's constant presence.

## History

**The Age of First Ice (Mythological):**
In the time before time, the primordial giant Ymir arose from the void. Unlike the Norse tales where Ymir dies, in Hrimgardian mythology Ymir transformed--becoming the great sky-giant whose body warms his children still. His three offspring orbit him: Hrimgard where mortals dwell, Jotunheim where the old giants remain, and Niflheim the corpse-realm where souls journey after death.

**The Three Homelands:**
* Dwarves settled Steinheimr, drawn by mineral wealth and defensible mountains
* Goblins claimed Varðland, finding the harsh north suited their hardy nature
* Giants established Fjallgard, the eastern mountains matching their scale

Each ancestry developed unique survival techniques. Over millennia, trade and intermarriage mixed the populations, but territorial naming and cultural identity persist.

**The Long Dark (Historical):**
Approximately 800 years ago, a volcanic eruption along the Spine triggered a decades-long winter that killed nearly half the population. The Long Dark is remembered in sagas as a time of terrible loss but also profound cooperation--the three ancestries sharing resources, shelter, and knowledge to survive. Modern Hrimgardian unity traces to bonds forged during the Long Dark.

**Xuartlek Connection:**
The mystery of why Xuartlek--the High City, the ascended sky tortle whose shell spans leagues--chose to anchor at Hrimgard remains unsolved. The frozen moon offers little obvious value compared to warmer, richer worlds. The people do not presume to understand; they are simply grateful.

**The Two Districts:**
* **Jorkarith**: Trade hub connecting to Khudealine (Langsevain) and Rodograd (Emerraine). Exports glacier-forged steel, ice-mead, gems.
* **Hallgerd**: Connects to Parthenay (Arborisle) and Novorzhev (Tertara). Cultural exchange focus, saga-sharing with other worlds.

**Trade:**
* **Exports**: Ice-mead, glacier-forged steel, gems, fish, wool, timber, runic crafts
* **Imports**: Tropical fruits, warm-weather textiles, spices, wood varieties that don't grow in cold
* **Most Precious Import**: The opportunity for Hrimgardian children to see warmer worlds--to know that water flows liquid somewhere, that green exists beyond black pine
