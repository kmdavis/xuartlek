## Description

Where survival depends on reading the sky and knowing when to move--a world that swings between fire and ice on a years-long pendulum, where clans migrate between highlands and valleys following orbital mechanics, and frozen warehouses carved into mountainsides hold the difference between life and starvation.

## Naming Conventions

Gaelic/Scottish influences throughout:
* **Strathis**: The star (Gaelic "strath" = river valley, broad valley)
* **Strafmack**: The world (Gaelic/invented compound, suggests "valley-plain" or "broad-plain")
* **Grianach**: Inner world (Gaelic = "sunny one")
* **Teasach**: Hot inner world (Gaelic = "hot")
* **Mòrchrios**: Gas giant (Gaelic = "great belt/girdle")
* **Gealach I-IV**: Moons of Mòrchrios (Gaelic = "moon")

* **Meat Runs**: Coming-of-age expeditions to frozen warehouses
* **Clan/Sept Structure**: Highland social organization adapted for survival

## Full Description

Strafmack is a world that refuses to let its people become comfortable. The planet traces a highly elliptical path around Strathis, creating multi-year cycles of habitability that have shaped every aspect of life here. The orbital eccentricity--unusually high at 0.16--produces dramatic variations in solar radiation and temperature measured not in months but in years. During perihelion, the "good years," high plateaus transform from frozen wastelands into livable grazing lands. During aphelion, the "hard years," those same plateaus become killing grounds.

The landscape is fundamentally vertical. Steep mountain ranges and high plateaus dominate the single supercontinent, with river valleys cutting deep channels through ancient rock. The aesthetic resembles the Scottish Highlands scaled up to continental proportions--rugged, misty when weather allows, beautiful in a way that can kill the unprepared. Settlements exist at multiple elevations: permanent valley towns where industry concentrates, mid-slope clanholds that serve as primary habitation, high plateau summer camps viable only during good years, and--most critical of all--the frozen warehouses carved into mountainsides at elevations calculated over generations.

These warehouses are more than storage; they're clan patrimony. Each is marked with ancestral symbols, positioned precisely where cold holds through even the warmest hard years, and defended with blood when necessary. Better positioning confers strategic advantage. Poor positioning means starvation. Throughout the hard years, young adults prove their courage on "meat runs"--expeditions ascending treacherous slopes to retrieve frozen stores while their clans huddle in valley settlements, sustained by bounty harvested during better times.

This is not nomadism. The people of Strafmack maintain permanent infrastructure at every elevation. But neither is it sedentary life. Strafmackish culture is defined by movement, by knowing when to ascend and when to retreat, by the bonds of clan and sept that keep extended families together through migrations spanning decades. The warehouses represent this duality--permanent infrastructure supporting a mobile people, built and maintained through coordinated efforts of multiple species working as one.

## Physical Characteristics

**Orbital Period:** ~1000 Strafmackish days (~3.54 Earth years)
**Rotation Period:** ~25 hours per day (250 Earth days = ~1 Strafmack year)
**Diameter:** 12,100 km (Earth-sized)
**Gravity:** Standard
**Atmosphere:** Breathable, temperate during good years, harsh during hard years
**Eccentricity:** 0.16 (highly eccentric orbit driving multi-year climate cycles)

**Climate:**
* **Perihelion ("Good Years")**: High plateaus become temperate, extensive grazing possible, limited farming viable
* **Aphelion ("Hard Years")**: High plateaus lethal, populations retreat to valleys
* **The Freezing**: Critical period when nighttime temps at warehouse elevation stay below freezing, triggering herd culling
* **Migration Timing**: Sacred knowledge passed through generations, tied to orbital position tracking
* **Temperature Swings**: Measured in years, not seasons; creates predictable but extreme variations

## Geography

**The Supercontinent:**
Single Pangaea-type landmass dominating the planet, characterized by extreme verticality and river valley systems cutting through steep terrain.

**Elevation Zones:**

**Valley Towns (Lowest Elevation):**
* Year-round habitation, industrial centers
* Forges, distilleries, workshops, cottage industries
* Water-powered mills augmented by bound elementals
* Steam technology supplemented by practical magic
* Trading hubs during good years, survival refuges during hard years

**Mid-Slope Clanholds (Primary Settlements):**
* Main clan territories, fortified communities
* Multi-species sept quarters clustered for defense and cooperation
* Clan councils, sept meeting halls, ancestral shrines
* Move between valley and plateau following orbital cycle
* Strategic elevation--accessible year-round but vulnerable during worst cold

**Frozen Warehouses (Critical Elevation):**
* Carved into mountainsides by Dwarf stone-masons
* Positioned where cold holds through warmest hard years
* Marked with clan ancestral symbols, defended as patrimony
* Natural freeze + ice magic + salt (triple redundancy)
* Source of meat runs during hard years

**High Plateau Summer Camps (Highest Elevation):**
* Only viable during perihelion approach
* Vast grazing lands, alpine meadows
* Temporary camps, seasonal herding operations
* Abandoned during descent toward aphelion
* Source of The Freezing's herds

**Geographic Features:**
* Steep mountain ranges resembling continental-scale Highlands
* Deep river valleys providing water and shelter
* Misty highlands, rugged beauty
* Avalanche zones (constant threat during meat runs)
* Natural cold pockets utilized for warehouse positioning

## Astronomical Features

**Orbital Mechanics:**

Strafmack's highly eccentric orbit (e = 0.16) drives the fundamental rhythm of life. The planet swings dramatically closer and farther from Strathis over its ~3.54 Earth-year orbital period, creating multi-year "good years" near perihelion and brutal "hard years" near aphelion.

**Perihelion (Good Years):**
* High solar radiation warms plateaus to habitable temperatures
* Grazing lands expand, limited farming possible
* Clans migrate upward in great seasonal movements
* Valley forges run hot producing migration tools
* Trade flourishes, marriages arranged, children born
* 18-24 month window of relative abundance

**Aphelion (Hard Years):**
* Solar radiation drops dramatically, plateaus become lethal
* Clans retreat to valleys, consolidate resources
* Frozen warehouses become lifelines
* Meat runs retrieve stores throughout winter
* Population survives on preserved food and valley agriculture
* 18-24 month window of scarcity and survival

**The Freezing Moon:**
No actual moon called this--it's a cultural term for the astronomical marker when nighttime temperatures at warehouse elevation consistently stay below freezing. Triggers the frantic herd culling season.

**Sky-Watching:**
* Dedicated clan members track stellar positions
* Calculate optimal migration windows
* Astronomy blended with tradition and survival necessity
* Moving too early = arrive before plateau habitable
* Moving too late = caught by hard years' onset
* Sacred knowledge passed through generations

**Mòrchrios (The Gas Giant):**
* Visible in night sky, dominates during certain seasons
* Cultural significance: "The Giant Who Scattered Our Moons"
* Four moons orbit the giant while Strafmack has none
* Folklore attributes Strafmack's eccentric orbit to ancient gravitational encounter
* Symbol of cosmic injustice--the giant kept its moons, scattered ours

## Culture

**Species (Pathfinder 2E):**
* **Dwarves** (20%): Forge-masters, miners, warehouse carvers. Ancient Heritage versatile. Form backbone of year-round valley settlements and maintain frozen warehouses. Essential sept role.
* **Goliaths** (20%): High plateau herders, meat run leaders. Elemental Heart (Ice) common. Embrace harsh migrations, lead expeditions during worst weather. Essential sept role.
* **Halflings** (20%): Valley farmers, brewers, stable communities. Hillock or Twilight versatile. Maintain food supplies when warehouses run low. Essential sept role.
* **Owlin** (15%): Scouts, messengers, weather watchers. Track weather patterns and avalanche conditions. Provide early warnings for meat runs. Essential sept role.
* **Minotaurs** (15%): Laborers, haulers, construction. Heavy lifting essential at all elevations--warehouse construction, meat hauling, culling season. Essential sept role.
* **Lizardfolk** (10%): Adapted to temperature extremes, unique torpor physiology. Enter torpor during coldest months reducing clan food burden, provide ice magic during The Freezing. Optional but valuable sept role.

**Clan and Sept Structure:**

**CLAN** (Political/Territorial Unit):
* Size: 100-300 people, comprising 5-10 septs
* Territory: Valley lands + warehouse complex + plateau grazing grounds
* Multi-species by necessity--survival demands all specializations
* Leadership: Council of sept elders, or dominant sept chieftain
* Examples: "Ironhorn Clan", "Brightforge Clan", "Deepvale Clan"
* Identity: Political alliance, shared infrastructure, military unit

**SEPT** (Family Unit):
* Size: 20-50 people, extended biological family
* Usually mono-species (exceptions from ancient mergers/adoptions)
* Specialized role within clan economy
* Examples:
  * "Steelforge Sept of Ironhorn" (Dwarves - forge masters, warehouse carvers)
  * "Highwatch Sept of Ironhorn" (Goliaths - herders, lead meat runs)
  * "Hearthkeep Sept of Ironhorn" (Halflings - valley farms, brewing)
  * "Skyward Sept of Ironhorn" (Owlin - scouts, weather watchers)
  * "Strongback Sept of Ironhorn" (Minotaurs - laborers, haulers)
  * "Coldscale Sept of Ironhorn" (Lizardfolk - ice-mages, torpor reserves)

**Clan Composition Requirements:**
A balanced clan needs Dwarf, Goliath, Halfling, Owlin, and Minotaur septs at minimum. Lizardfolk septs provide additional resilience. No mono-species clan has survived long-term.

**Social Dynamics:**
* Within-clan marriages (between septs): Common, strengthen unity
* Cross-clan marriages: Major political alliances, often involve warehouse access
* Adopted orphans: From destroyed clans, become full sept members
* Sworn bonds: Unrelated individuals adopted into sept (rare, deeply honored)
* Conservative clans: Mostly mono-species septs, viewed as less resilient
* Failed septs: Absorbed by related septs within same clan

**The Freezing:**

The most critical period in Strafmackish culture. When nighttime temperatures at warehouse elevation stay consistently below freezing, entire clans mobilize to cull herds down to breeding stock and preserve meat before spoilage.

**The Process:**
1. Goliath herders drive animals down from plateaus
2. Dwarf butchers process carcasses with industrial efficiency
3. Halfling preservationists manage salting and preparation
4. Lizardfolk ice-mages reinforce the freeze with magic
5. Minotaur laborers haul tons of meat up to warehouses
6. Owlin scouts monitor weather and coordinate logistics

**Timing:**
* Wait too long = animals die naturally or become too weak
* Move too early = risk of spoilage before freeze holds
* 2-3 week window of 24/7 activity
* Entire sept mobilization, children included in age-appropriate tasks
* Combines urgency, grief for herds, and grim determination

**Cultural Significance:**
* Failure = clan starvation
* Success requires flawless sept coordination
* Rite of passage: first Freezing participation marks adulthood threshold
* Stories passed down: "The Year Ironhorn Processed Two Thousand"

**Meat Runs:**

Throughout hard years, expeditions retrieve frozen stores from warehouses. These are coming-of-age journeys testing courage, endurance, and judgment.

**Frequency:** Every 3-6 weeks depending on clan size and stores
**Duration:** 2-7 days depending on elevation and weather
**Composition:** Mixed-sept teams (Goliath leadership, Owlin scouts, Minotaur haulers, Dwarf technical experts)
**Risks:** Avalanches, extreme cold, predators, rival clans, equipment failure

**Timing Calculation:**
* Early winter: Easier travel, less certain freeze
* Deep winter: Brutal conditions, guaranteed frozen stores
* Risk vs reward judgment essential

**Cultural Impact:**
* Success earns respect across all septs
* Failure rarely survived to be judged
* Tragic runs become cautionary tales: "The Year the Ironhorn Lost Ten"
* Every clan maintains memorial walls for lost runners

**Frozen Warehouse Protection:**
* Theft = gravest clan offense (survival theft)
* Defended with blood when necessary
* Marriages sometimes negotiated around warehouse access
* Disasters: avalanche destruction, unseasonable warmth, raiders
* Cross-clan raids during desperate hard years spark multi-generation feuds

**Magic Level:** Moderate
* Practical integration into daily life
* Elemental binding (water, fire) common in valleys
* Ice magic essential for warehouse preservation
* Magic more reliable than pure technology in highlands
* Enchanted cold-weather gear, fire spirits for warmth
* Divination for weather prediction and avalanche warning

**Technology Level:** Early Industrial Revolution (Magic-Augmented)
* Valley industry: water wheels + water elementals
* Steam engines supplement magical solutions
* Coal-fired forges + fire spirit bindings
* Metallurgy: Dwarf smithing tradition + industrial techniques
* Cottage industries: weaving, brewing, preservation
* Triple redundancy philosophy: natural + magical + technological

**Calendar Importance:**
* Tracks orbital position as much as seasons
* Major festivals mark perihelion/aphelion passages
* Migration timing is sacred knowledge
* Dedicated sky-watchers calculate optimal windows
* Children learn astronomical navigation from early age
* "Good years" vs "Hard years" terminology fundamental

**Social Values:**
* Hardy individualism: self-sufficiency required for survival
* Fierce clan/sept loyalty: extended family bonds
* Multi-species cooperation: species purity is foolish luxury
* Sept specialization: everyone knows their role
* Movement as virtue: staying put is death
* Warehouse sanctity: theft worse than murder

**Theme:** Highland Transhumance Fantasy with Orbital Survival Mechanics

## History

**The Ancient Scattering (Mythological):**

Folklore claims Strafmack once had moons like Mòrchrios, but the gas giant's gravitational interference scattered them during a close encounter millennia ago, simultaneously perturbing Strafmack's orbit into its current eccentric path. The story is told to explain why the giant kept four beautiful moons while Strafmack has none--cosmic injustice written in the sky.

No historical records verify this. But the orbital mechanics are real, and something caused the eccentricity. Astronomers debate whether a gravitational encounter could produce these results, but without moons to study, the truth is lost to time.

**The First Warehouses:**

Oral tradition credits the legendary "First Clans"--Dwarf stone-masons and Goliath herders working together--with carving the original frozen warehouses approximately 2,000 years ago. Before this innovation, aphelion meant massive population die-offs. The warehouses enabled survival, but required multi-species cooperation. This historical necessity is why mono-species clans are viewed with suspicion--ancestors learned the hard way that survival demands all specializations.

**The Long Generations:**

Strafmackish history is measured in orbital cycles rather than individual lifetimes. The "Long Generations" period (roughly 1,000-500 years ago) saw clan territories stabilize, sept structures formalize, and migration patterns optimize through trial, error, and countless deaths. This period established the cultural frameworks that persist today.

**The Meat Run Tragedies:**

Every clan maintains records of failed meat runs, passed down as cautionary tales:
* **"The Year the Ironhorn Lost Ten"**: Highwatch Sept meat run caught by sudden avalanche, wiped out ten of their strongest
* **"When Brightforge Found the Avalanche"**: Entire run buried, discovered only when spring melt revealed the bodies
* **"The Deepvale Starvation"**: Warehouse destroyed by unseasonable thaw, clan reduced from 200 to 40 by spring

These stories aren't morbid--they're survival education. Each tragedy taught lessons about timing, weather reading, route selection, and risk calculation.

**The Clan Wars:**

Approximately 300 years ago, a series of brutal hard years triggered widespread warehouse raids as desperate clans attacked neighbors' stores. The "Clan Wars" lasted two full orbital cycles (7+ Earth years) and reduced the population by approximately one-third. The conflict ended not through peace treaties but through mutual exhaustion and recognition that inter-clan violence threatened total extinction.

The Wars established modern warehouse defense protocols and the principle that theft of frozen stores is a capital offense recognized by all clans. They also led to the practice of cross-clan marriages as alliance building--better to share resources through marriage than spill blood over them.

**Xuartlek Connection:**

**Ciliren District:** Founded 430 IR in a mid-elevation valley that remains habitable year-round. The location was strategic--accessible during both good and hard years, demonstrating Xuartlek's understanding of orbital mechanics.

**First Contact Impact:**
Portal arrival introduced Strafmack to worlds that found the orbital cycle baffling. "Why not just settle permanently in the valleys?" outsiders asked. The question itself revealed ignorance--because during good years, the high plateaus offer resources valleys cannot match. To abandon that opportunity would be to abandon half of what the world offers.

**Trade:**
* **Exports**: Preserved meats (frozen warehouse techniques), cold-weather gear, enchanted survival equipment, Dwarf metalwork, clan-bonded mercenaries (sept units that work seamlessly together), orbital navigation expertise
* **Imports**: Raw materials, magical components, luxury foods (fresh fruits unknown on Strafmack), technological innovations, scholarly knowledge

**Cultural Exchange:**
Ciliren serves as a pressure valve during hard years--wealthier clans can purchase imported food to supplement warehouse stores, reducing starvation risk. This has created economic stratification previously unknown. Clans with good portal trade relationships weather hard years more comfortably; clans without such connections struggle as ancestors did.

Some conservative clans view this as cheating--survival should depend on proper preparation, not external resources. Others embrace the advantage. The debate continues, but few refuse portal trade when starvation threatens.

**Astronomical Validation:**
Xuartlek scholars confirmed what Strafmackish sky-watchers long suspected--the eccentric orbit is stable on multi-million-year timescales, not a temporary aberration. This knowledge settled theological debates about whether the "Long Winter" (a prophesied eternal aphelion) would come. It won't. The cycle is permanent, and Strafmack's people are adapted to it perfectly.
