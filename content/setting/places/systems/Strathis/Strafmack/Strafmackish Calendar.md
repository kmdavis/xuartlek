## Description

A calendar built for orbital survival--where the year is divided not by seasons but by orbital position, and the clan calendars track not just days but the life-or-death timing of vertical migrations across a world of extreme orbital eccentricity.

## Overview

- **World**: [[Strafmack]] in the Strathis System
- **Theme**: Scottish Highlands fantasy, orbital transhumance, clan survival
- **Year Length**: 250 days (10 months × 25 days)
- **Day Length**: 31 hours
- **Orbital Cycle**: 4 local years = 1000 days = full orbit (~3.54 Earth years)

## Orbital Mechanics

### Eccentric Orbit
- Orbital eccentricity: ~0.16 (highly elliptical for a habitable world)
- Complete orbit: 1000 local days = 4 local years
- Temperature variation measured in years, not months

### Climate Effects
- **At perihelion**: High plateaus become temperate, extensive grazing possible
- **At aphelion**: High plateaus become death traps, retreat to valleys required
- **Seasonal lag**: Planet takes time to warm/cool after orbital position changes

## Structure

### Basic Parameters
- **Weeks**: 5 days (Gaelic Di- pattern)
- **Months**: 10 months of 25 days each
- **Weeks per Month**: 5 (clean division: 25 ÷ 5)
- **No Leap Days**: Perfect 1000-day orbit requires no intercalation

### Days of the Week (Gaelic/Highland)
Named for vertical geography of transhumance culture:
1. **Digleann** (Vale Day): Valley work, lowland activities
2. **Didùn** (Hold Day): Fortified settlements, clan business
3. **Diabhainn** (River Day): Water management, transport, trade
4. **Diàrd** (Plateau Day): High altitude work, herding
5. **Difois** (Rest Day): Rest, clan gatherings, market day

### Day Structure
- 31 hours per day
- Sleep cycle: ~10 hours
- Awake time: ~20 hours (with mid-day nap)
- Work shifts vary by sept: Dwarves often work 2 shifts, Halflings one long shift

## The Four-Year Orbital Cycle

### Year Numbering
Year numbering tracks orbital position:
- **Year 1 of cycle**: Perihelion (peak good year, plateaus warming)
- **Year 2 of cycle**: Descending (The Freezing occurs, transition to hard years)
- **Year 3 of cycle**: Aphelion (peak hard year, deep valleys only)
- **Year 4 of cycle**: Ascending (warming begins, prepare for The Climb)

### The Turning (Perihelion)
Major festival marking when orbit begins return journey:
- Celebrates beginning of good years
- Clans prepare for ascending migration
- Season of marriages, births, optimism

### The Dimming (Aphelion)
Somber observance marking when orbit reaches farthest point:
- Beginning of hard years
- Final preparations for valley consolidation
- Focus on survival, conservation, clan bonds

## Transhumance Migrations

### Settlement Pattern
- **Valley towns**: Permanent, year-round. Industry, magic-tech, population centers during hard years
- **Mid-slope clanholds**: Primary clan settlements, fortified, some seasonal
- **High plateau camps**: Summer grazing grounds, only habitable during perihelion years

### The Climb (Ascending Migration)
Occurs gradually as perihelion approaches:
- Timing is sacred: too early = death by cold, too late = lost opportunity
- Herds driven to high plateau grazing lands
- Extended families travel together
- Can take weeks to complete ascent
- Celebrated when each clan reaches their high camp

### The Return (Descending Migration)
More urgent than The Climb:
- Race against approaching hard years
- Consolidation into valleys and mid-slope holds
- Herds reduced to breeding stock
- Relief when valley is reached safely

## The Freezing Economy

### Natural Refrigeration
The most critical survival adaptation exploits orbital cooling for food preservation.

### The Freezing Moon
Calendar marker for culling season:
- Month when nighttime temperatures at warehouse elevation stay below freezing
- Sky-watchers monitor temperatures and orbital position carefully
- Marks transition from descent to full hard years

### Culling Week(s)
Intensive processing period (2-3 weeks):
- Begins when The Freezing is confirmed (several nights of hard freeze)
- Entire clans mobilize: hunters, butchers, preservationists work in shifts
- Herds reduced from hundreds/thousands to essential breeding stock
- Triple preservation: natural freeze + ice magic + salt
- Atmosphere combines urgency, grief (for the herds), and grim determination
- Success = survival; failure = starvation

### First Run
Ceremonial first meat retrieval (~4-6 weeks after culling):
- Proves the warehouses held, meat is still frozen
- Led by experienced adults, teaches younger generation the route
- Major relief when meat is confirmed frozen and unspoiled

### Meat Run Schedule
Regular retrieval expeditions throughout hard years:
- Frequency: Every 3-6 weeks depending on clan size
- Early winter runs: Easier travel, less certain freeze (younger parties)
- Mid-winter runs: Brutal conditions, guaranteed frozen stores (experienced teams)
- Late winter runs: Race against thaw, last retrievals before good years return
- Each run noted in calendar: "Third Meat Run of Hard Year 47"
- Failed runs become calendar markers: "The Year the Ironhorn Lost Ten"

### Warehouse Sealing
Post-culling ritual:
- Warehouses magically and physically sealed
- Clan elders perform blessing ceremonies
- Defensive wards placed against raiders and scavengers
- Opening outside proper meat runs = severe taboo

## Social Structure Integration

### Clans and Septs
- **Clan**: Political/territorial unit (100-300 people, multi-species)
- **Sept**: Family unit within clan (20-50 people, usually mono-species, specialized role)
- Example: "Highwatch Sept of Ironhorn Clan" (Goliaths who lead meat runs)
- Survival requires all sept specializations: no mono-species clans exist
- Calendar tracking is clan-level (migration timing, meat runs, festivals)

### Calendar Events
- Coming-of-age ceremonies tied to first successful meat run
- Marriage negotiations during First Run feast (proof of clan viability)
- Warehouse inheritance ceremonies when elders die (passing clan patrimony)
- Memorial days for failed meat runs (clan-specific, deeply solemn)
- Culling festivals: mixture of grief, celebration, and desperate hope

## Cultural Significance

### Calendar Expertise
- Sky-watchers and clan historians highly valued
- Migration timing decisions are collective (calendar + observation)
- Different clans use slight variations, but core structure is shared

### Time Measurement
On Strafmack, time is measured in orbital position as much as days. "Three hard years ago" means something specific about orbital mechanics. The calendar isn't just tracking time--it's tracking survival probability.
