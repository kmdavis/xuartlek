STRATHIS SYSTEM - Scottish Highlands Fantasy

WORLD: Strafmack - A world of hardy individualism, steep mountains, and high plateaus

ASTRONOMICAL CURIOSITY: Highly Eccentric Elliptical Orbit
- Strafmack has unusually elliptical orbit (eccentricity ≈ 0.16)
- Creates dramatic multi-year seasonal variations in temperature and solar radiation
- Perihelion (close approach): high plateaus become temperate, farming possible
- Aphelion (far approach): extreme cold forces migration to lower altitudes
- Orbital period creates "good years" (near perihelion) and "hard years" (aphelion)

CULTURAL ADAPTATION - TRANSHUMANCE, NOT NOMADISM:
- **Permanent settlements** at multiple elevations:
* Valley towns: year-round habitation, industry, forges, distilleries
* Mid-slope clanholds: primary settlements, move between valley and plateau
* High plateau summer camps: only viable during perihelion years
* Frozen warehouses: carved into rock at critical elevations (clan patrimony)
- **Seasonal migration** following orbital mechanics:
* Clans move UP during perihelion "good years" (high plateaus warm enough)
* Clans move DOWN during aphelion "hard years" (consolidate in valleys)
* Transhumance patterns are predictable, tied to orbital position
- **Magic-Industrial Hybrid Economy**:
* Valley industry: water wheels + water elementals, forges + fire spirits
* Steam tech in lowlands supplemented by practical magic
* Cottage industries in clanholds: weaving, smithing, brewing
* High plateau herding during good years
* Magic more reliable than coal in highlands

THE NATURAL REFRIGERATION ECONOMY:
Strafmackish survival depends on exploiting the planet's orbital cycle for food preservation:
- **The Freezing**: Critical calendar marker when nighttime temps stay below freezing
* Occurs at warehouse elevation during descent toward aphelion
* Clans don't cull herds until The Freezing arrives (risk of spoilage)
* Waiting too long = animals die naturally or become too weak
* 2-3 week window of intense activity: entire herds processed
- **Frozen Warehouses**: Sacred clan infrastructure
* Carved into rock or built at elevations above mid-slope clanholds
* Marked with ancestral symbols, clan patrimony passed through generations
* Preservation: natural freeze + ice magic + salt (triple redundancy for survival)
* Better positioning = strategic clan advantage
- **Meat Runs**: Throughout hard years, parties ascend to retrieve frozen stores
* Coming-of-age tradition: young adults proving courage and capability
* Risk calculation: early winter (easier travel, less certain freeze) vs
deep winter (brutal conditions, guaranteed frozen stores)
* Typically every 3-6 weeks depending on clan size and stores
* Failed runs become tragic clan history: "The Year the Ironhorn Lost Ten"
- **Social Implications**:
* Theft of frozen stores = gravest clan offense (survival theft)
* Marriages sometimes negotiated around warehouse access/sharing
* Disasters: avalanche destroys stores, unseasonable warmth, raiders
* Warehouse defense during hard years can lead to clan feuds

CLAN AND SEPT STRUCTURE:
Strafmackish society follows traditional Highland structure adapted for survival:

**CLAN** (larger political/territorial unit, multi-species):
- Size: 100-300 people, comprising 5-10 septs
- Territory: Owns valley lands + warehouse complex + plateau grazing grounds
- Multi-species by necessity: survival demands all specializations
- Leadership: Council of sept elders, or dominant sept chieftain
- Examples: "Ironhorn Clan", "Brightforge Clan", "Deepvale Clan"
- Identity: Political alliance, shared survival infrastructure, military unit

**SEPT** (smaller family unit, usually mono-species):
- Size: 20-50 people, extended biological family
- Part of larger clan structure
- Usually mono-species (some exceptions from ancient mergers/adoptions)
- Specialized role within clan economy
- Examples:
* "Steelforge Sept of Ironhorn" (Dwarves - forge masters, warehouse carvers)
* "Highwatch Sept of Ironhorn" (Goliaths - herders, lead meat runs)
* "Hearthkeep Sept of Ironhorn" (Halflings - valley farms, brewing)
* "Skyward Sept of Ironhorn" (Owlin - scouts, weather watchers)
* "Strongback Sept of Ironhorn" (Minotaurs - laborers, haulers)
**TYPICAL CLAN COMPOSITION**:
A balanced clan needs:
- Dwarf sept(s): Forges, mines, warehouse construction/maintenance
- Goliath sept(s): High plateau herding, meat run leadership
- Halfling sept(s): Valley agriculture, brewing, stable food supply
- Owlin sept(s): Scouting, messages, weather/avalanche warning
- Minotaur sept(s): Heavy labor, hauling frozen meat, construction
- Lizardfolk sept(s) (optional): Adaptable to extremes, torpor reduces food needs

**SOCIAL DYNAMICS**:
- Within-clan marriages (between septs): Common, strengthen clan unity
- Cross-clan marriages: Major political alliances, often involve territory/warehouse access
- Failed septs: Absorbed by related septs within same clan
- Sept extinction: "The Year the Ironhorn Lost Ten" = Highwatch Sept tragedy
- Adopted orphans: From destroyed clans, become full sept members
- Sworn bonds: Unrelated individuals adopted into sept (rare, deeply honored)
- Multi-species septs: Exist from ancient mergers, seen as either cosmopolitan or chaotic
- Conservative clans: Mostly mono-species septs, viewed as less resilient

**INHERITANCE AND PROPERTY**:
- Warehouses: Clan property, specific septs maintain/guard them
- Territory: Clan holdings, septs have traditional use rights
- Sept property: Tools, herds, personal possessions stay within sept
- Chieftainship: Usually inherited within dominant sept, but can shift

CULTURAL TRAITS:
- Hardy individualism: self-sufficiency required for survival
- Strong clan/sept loyalty: extended family units that migrate together
- Orbital calendar: tracks perihelion/aphelion as much as seasons
- "Good years" vs "Hard years" terminology fundamental to culture
- Migration timing is sacred: too early = death by cold, too late = starvation
- Sept specialization: each knows their role in clan survival
- Multi-species cooperation: survival demands it, species purity is foolish luxury
