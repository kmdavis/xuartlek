THE MYTH OF THE FIRST TREE (what people believe):

* Lughna: Too close to the sun, scorched clean of blessing

THE MYTH OF THE FIRST TREE (what people believe):
* The First Tree is the mythical progenitor of all forests, roots reaching through reality itself
* Where its roots touch, life flourishes; where they withdraw, worlds remain barren
* Lughna: Too close to the sun, scorched clean of blessing
* Brigantia: Red goddess world, thin air, harsh seasons--blessing barely reaches
* Tiroedd: The "waiting lands"--abandoned after the First Betrayal, or blessing deliberately withdrawn as warning
* Arborisle: Deep blessing, ancient forests fed by First Tree's roots
* Boskara: Garden-moon planted by ancient elves "before the sundering," transforming into second paradise
* Aeloria: The Patient watcher, grey and still
* Dagdarion: The Father, ancient patience embodied in amber storms
* Morrígan: The Fate-Weaver--will wake when Boskara's transformation completes (prophecy)

THE SCIENTIFIC REALITY (not in public narrative):
* NAMING: Celtic/Gaelic throughout. "Sylvoria" = realm of woods (ancient elvish)
* Lughna: Scorched innermost world, too hot for life
* Brigantia: Hot Mars-like world at inner edge of habitable zone, thin atmosphere
* Tiroedd: Temperate, Earth-like conditions but uninhabited (no ruins found from orbit--genuine mystery)
* Arborisle: Cool forested world at outer edge of habitable zone, perpetual mists/fog
* Boskara: "Living moon" seeded by ancient elves (pre-High/Wood split) millennia ago
- Common belief: Nearly habitable paradise waiting to be claimed
- Visible: Eerie green glow from spore-clouds in thick atmosphere
- 28-day cycle: Important to druidic calendars
- [SPOILER: Actually extremely hostile--aggressive spores strip flesh to bone in hours. Only a few scholars know.]
* Aeloria: Smaller lifeless companion moon, grey rock
* Asteroid Belt: Standard debris ring separating inner/outer system
* Dagdarion: Gas giant, amber-colored, Jupiter-analog with ancient storm systems
* Morrígan: Ice giant, Neptune-analog, named for Celtic goddess of fate
