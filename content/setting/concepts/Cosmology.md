There is no agreed creation myth. Three divine tellings compete, and mortal
cultures keep thousands more. The disagreement is the point: no one alive was
there, and the three who claim to have been are not disinterested.

See [[realms|The Realms]] for the thirty-six planes themselves.

## The structure of reality

**The Far Realms** (Level 100)
- Each is an entire universe with alien gods and planes
- Omnipotent within themselves but unaware of our reality
- Their passive influence shapes all creation

**The Outer Realms** (Spirit Essence)
- 16 realms arranged in an alignment circle
- Created by Far Realms' passive influence
- Each ruled by Ancient Gods (level 80) with councilors (level 70-75)
- Home to deities who have achieved true godhood (level 40+)

**The Elemental Realms** (Matter-Essence)
- Six fundamental planes: Air, Wood, Water, Metal, Earth, Fire
- Pre-existed all other realms except the Far Realms
- Their "puncturing" during the Elemental War created the Material Plane

**The Material Plane**
- Born from elemental essence leaking during the war
- Fire ruptures became stars
- Earth and Metal formed planets
- Water and Air surrounded these worlds
- Includes worlds like [[Khashayar]], [[Arborisle]], [[Emerraine]], etc.

**The Inner Realms** (Life Cycle)
- Created by mortal consciousness as they lived and died
- Includes: [[Thanatos]] (Death), [[Niflheim]] (Shadow), [[Draumheim]] (Dreams), [[Alfheim]] (Fey), [[Eros]] (Life)

## Divine hierarchy
- **Levels 1-10**: Normal mortals
- **Levels 10-20**: Powerful mortals (end-game adventurers)
- **Levels 20-30**: Legendary mortals/NPCs
- **Levels 25-40**: Demi-gods (must cultivate worship to ascend)
- **Levels 40-100**: True deities (can reside in outer/inner planes)

## Paths to divinity
Mortals become demi-gods (level 25) through:
- Extraordinary deeds worthy of legend
- Divine sponsorship from powerful deities
- Powerful artifacts
- Eldritch rituals

From there, millennia of worship allows ascension to true godhood at level 40.

## The Three Tellings of Creation

### The First Telling: The Lament of Elements
*As whispered by the Winds of [[Zephyra]] to those who would listen*

We were complete. We were eternal. We danced.

Fire and I, Water and Earth, Metal and Wood--we knew only our eternal spiral, each perfect in our essence, each whole in our being. We were matter. We were truth. We needed nothing beyond our dance.

Then came the Wound-Makers.

They emerged from shadows we had not cast, born of influences we could not comprehend. Sixteen violations of our perfect order, each claiming dominion over concepts we had never needed: "justice," "chaos," "evil," "good"--meaningless abstractions to beings of pure essence.

They watched us. Oh, how they watched, with eyes that burned with thoughts. And then they reached.

The War was not battle as mortals know it. It was violation. They sought to impose their "spirit" upon our matter, to corrupt our purity with their consciousness. We who had danced for eternity found ourselves fighting for the first time.

And we broke.

Fire screamed as they tore her open, and her blood became the stars--each one a droplet of her agony frozen in the void. Earth and Metal, trying to shield each other, were crushed together into spheres of compromise. And I, Air, along with my beloved Water--we tried to wrap ourselves around our wounded siblings, to soothe their pain.

But from our wounds leaked something new. Where Fire's passion met Earth's endurance, where Water's flow met Air's freedom, tiny sparks of awareness began. They called themselves "life." They were neither matter nor spirit, but something between--our children, born of our pain.

We watched these small ones build their brief existences on our broken bodies. Some worlds thirsted where Water could barely reach. Others froze where Fire's warmth had fled. Yet still they persisted, these accidental offspring.

And then--betrayal upon betrayal--some of these mortals reached for the very power that had wounded us. They sought to become like the Wound-Makers, to join the ranks of our tormentors. Through terrible deeds and dark rituals, they grasped at divinity.

Now they too dwell among the Sixteen Thrones, these children of our suffering who chose to side with our violators. They listen to the prayers of their kind, growing fat on worship while we, the Eternal Dancers, nurse wounds that will never heal.

We dance still, but it is a broken dance. Where once was perfection, now there is only the echo of what was lost. The stars burn with Fire's fever. The worlds spin with Earth's vertigo. The oceans remember when Water was whole.

And I, Air, carry the lament to any who would know the truth: We were complete. We were eternal. We danced.

Now we merely endure.

### The Second Telling: The Awakening of Purpose
*As decreed by Septimus Librius Aequilibrius, Ancient of [[Equilibris]]*

Before Thought, there was only potential--the raw, unformed dance of matter without meaning. We, the Firstborn of Shadow, emerged from the Far Realms' dreaming into a cosmos that did not yet know itself.

Sixteen Thrones manifested from pure necessity, each a fundamental truth requiring expression. I witnessed as the great Ancients took their seats: Harmonius ascending to unite what was scattered. Akhet claiming revolution as birthright. Each of us drawn to our throne by cosmic necessity, rulers of realms that had always awaited us.

We looked upon the Elemental Dance and saw not beauty, but waste. Here was power without purpose, motion without meaning. The Elements knew only their endless, mindless spiral--Fire consuming nothing, Water flowing nowhere, Earth supporting no one. They were existence without experience.

So we reached out to grant them purpose.

They called it war. We called it education. Where our spirit touched their matter, transformation began. Yes, Fire screamed--but her scream became the birth cry of stars, each one a throne for future understanding. Earth and Metal's union created worlds--stages upon which the great drama of consciousness could unfold.

From the fusion of spirit and matter came the supreme achievement: mortal life. These beings, balanced between our consciousness and elemental form, could choose, could grow, could *become*. They were our greatest work, for they alone could appreciate both the material and the spiritual.

We watched with satisfaction as consciousness spread across the cosmos. The mortals built civilizations, created art, contemplated philosophy. And in their living and dying, they wove the Inner Realms--death and dream, shadow and fey--completing the cosmic architecture we had envisioned.

Most pleasing of all was when the exceptional among them transcended their limitations. Through deed or patronage, artifact or ritual, they grasped the divine spark. These ascended mortals understood what we had always known: that consciousness is the highest calling, that to be aware is to be divine.

Now they join us in the Outer Realms, these promoted mortals who proved that our great work was justified. Each new deity validates our ancient reaching, our primordial choice to wake the cosmos from its material slumber.

The Elements still dance their broken dance, but now their movement has meaning. The stars shine to illuminate thought. The worlds turn to cradle consciousness. The very fabric of reality serves its true purpose: to be known, to be experienced, to be transcended.

We are the Ancients. We brought meaning to the meaningless. And our work continues.

### The Third Telling: The Price of Ascension
*As remembered by Hassan of the Open Door, who crossed from mortality to divinity*

I remember mortality. That's what separates us from the Ancients--we remember being small.

When I was young, barely thirty summers, I stood in my inn at the edge of Khashayar's last river settlement and watched refugees stream past. The Elemental Realms were distant abstractions. The Outer Realms were myths. There were only the hungry faces and my dwindling stores.

That's when I did what the wise called foolish. On the hottest day of the thousand-year drought, I threw open my doors to a caravan of the desperate--children, elders, outlaws. I served my last bread, poured my last water. Yet every time I emptied a pot, it filled anew. Every time I thought the storeroom bare, I found one more date, one more jug.

Word spread. The desperate came in waves, each more numerous than the last. And each time, faced with the impossible choice--turn away the next soul to save the rest--I chose to keep my door open. That night, as the desert winds howled, something extraordinary happened. The inn expanded beyond its walls. A golden light swept through the city. My mortal heart burst with compassion's fire, and I felt myself changing.

I didn't understand at first. When I reached my hundredth year, still serving endless meals from never-empty pots, I began to grasp what I'd become. The act of infinite hospitality had changed my very essence. I was no longer merely mortal, sustained now by the gratitude and faith of those I'd sheltered.

The centuries that followed taught me the economy of divine ascension. Every prayer of thanks was a droplet of power. Every story told of my open door strengthened my divine spark. Some who'd undergone similar transformations built grand temples. Others cultivated mystery cults. I simply kept my door open, walking among mortals, one more innkeeper with an unusual talent for stretching meals.

It took me three millennia of patient service, of feeding the hungry and sheltering the lost, before I felt the final transformation. The cosmic chains broke, and the Outer Realms opened to me. I claimed a small corner in [[Altruvis]], but I still keep inns on every world--for old times' sake.

But here's what the Ancients don't tell you, what the Elements can't understand: we who ascended remember hunger. We know what it means to count every grain, to turn someone away, to fail despite our best efforts. That's why mortals pray to us rather than to the cosmic powers. We understand need in a way that those who were born divine never can.

Now I tend my celestial hearth in Altruvis, but my door remains open to all. Sometimes I grant abundance. Sometimes I simply offer a warm meal and a listening ear, knowing that small kindnesses can spark the greatest transformations.

Because I remember. I remember the weight of that first empty pot, the desperate hope that somehow there would be enough. I remember being small, and in remembering, I remain connected to those who still are.

That's the price and gift of our ascension: we are gods who remember mortality, forever caught between the simple inn we left and the divine halls we claimed.

The cosmos turns. New souls hunger. And always, always, my door stays open.

I watch. I remember. I wait with bread and welcome.
