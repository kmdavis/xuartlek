# Portal Frame

An arcane device creating a stable, bidirectional portal between two distant frames,
which may be on different worlds. Universally shortened to **portal**.

Built and maintained by [[The High City Factions|the Academy]], which has held that authority
since it built the first one in 090 and had it written into the Concord in 390.

## The structure

Two very tall towers, rising from below ground to a thousand feet.

| | |
|---|---|
| Gap at ground level | **100 ft**, wide enough for a modestly sized sailing ship |
| Gap at the top | **200 ft**, wide enough for the largest airship |
| Height of the frames | **1,000 ft** |
| Apex of the portal arc | **1,200 ft** |

The towers start perpendicular to the ground and lean apart as they rise. Below
ground, pipes and conduits carry fluids between the two worlds.

## Who passes where

| Height | Traffic |
|---|---|
| Ground | People and wagons. **A toll is paid before entering.** Some portals carry a canal on one side for sailing ships |
| 300 ft | Aerial trams, both sides |
| 500 ft and above | Airships |

**The toll booths at the foot of every portal fund the entire imperial state.** There
is no head tax, no property tax and no sales tax. See [[Government of Xuartlek]].

## Anchors, and the two kinds of door

A portal frame is a **ship-gauge** connection and needs **an anchor at both ends**.
Anchors are physical, transportable at ruinous cost, and building one where none
exists is a multi-year engineering project. Rovigo's anchor was hauled thousands of
miles overland across a magic-dead world by steam locomotive, and took five years.

The **research rings** held in the Under City are a different thing entirely:
person-gauge, one traveller and what they can carry, and they need no hardware
waiting on the far side. They can open a door to any world, known or unknown, for a
few hours. They cannot pass a ship, a horse, or a crate of any size.

That distinction is the reason freight still moves slowly and legally through the
registered network, and it is the whole business model of anyone who leases a ring.

## Gauge

A hull must fit the aperture, and hulls are rated by **portal gauge**: the diameter
of the circle everything must fit inside once masts are struck and spars are swept.
A mid-sized amphibious freighter runs to about 95 ft, leaving 12.5 ft of clearance
all round in a 120 ft aperture.

> **Unresolved:** that 120 ft working aperture comes from the naval architecture
> notes and does not obviously match the 200 ft apex gap above. Either the usable
> aperture is narrower than the frame spacing, or the two figures were written at
> different times and one is wrong.

## Failure

A portal can be destroyed, and doing so severs the connection permanently unless the
frame is rebuilt and the anchors survive. **This has happened once.** Duathamper's
portal was destroyed in 384 by the defenders themselves, to stop an invasion reaching
the High City. The district on the far side fell two years later. Whether the
High City's anchor survived is not recorded.

Three research rings have also been "lost to shear effect" over the centuries, in
285, 355 and 390.
