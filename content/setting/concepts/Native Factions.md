# Native Factions

A framework for inventing factions on any Low City world in about five minutes,
without contradicting anybody. Everything beyond the Low City is a blank slate on
purpose; this is how to fill it in.

For the imperial factions, which are settled canon, see
[[The High City Factions]].

Everything beyond the Low City is a blank slate on purpose. This is how to fill it
in about five minutes without contradicting anybody.

## The premise every world shares

Xuartlek arrives. He stays roughly thirty years. Surveyors pick a site, a district
is built, a portal opens. Then he leaves, and **the portal stays**.

So every world in the Low City has been permanently rearranged by a doorway it did
not ask for, cannot close, and does not administer. Trade reoriented, the calendar
resynchronised to Imperial Time and never went back, and a piece of another
empire's law now sits inside its borders forever.

Native factions are the answer to what that did to the people who were already
there.

## The rule that keeps it thematic

> **A native faction should be defined by an opinion about its neighbours, not by
> an opinion about the empire.**

Factions built on imperial stances produce debates. Factions built on local
grievances that the portal made urgent produce **jobs** -- someone to rob, someone
to protect, someone to find, somewhere to be before a rival gets there.

It also keeps the empire as weather rather than as a question the players are
asked to answer, which is what the table has asked for.

## The five positions

Nearly every world sorts into these. Reskin, rename, and vary which ones matter.

**1. The Brokers.** Natives who obtained citizenship, by service or by purchase,
and now stand between the district and the world. Rich, indispensable, and
resented by both sides. Structurally disloyal to everyone, because their whole
value is being the only ones who can talk to both. Every world has these.

**2. The Demoted.** Whoever ran things before the district was founded. Not
necessarily poor. *Demoted*, which stings more. Their titles still exist and no
longer decide anything. They remember a currency nobody accepts and a calendar
nobody keeps.

**3. The Hinterland.** The villages, clans, guilds or states in the district's
shadow that feed it, haul for it, and take its money without any say in it.
Biggest population, no representation, and the source of every rising the empire
has ever had to put down.

**4. The Enlisted.** Those taking the uniform, because service is the only route
to citizenship most people will ever have. Loyal to the empire in a way other
natives find unforgivable and born citizens find insufficient. Their families are
proud and quiet about it.

**5. The Departed.** Those who went through the portal and did not come back, and
what grew up around their absence: waiting families, remittance economies, and
cults of the Wanderer. Xuartlek's domains are Magic, Travel, Cities and Knowledge,
and he accrues worshippers on worlds he left a century ago.

## Three modifiers

**Age.** How long since he came? One generation and the wound is fresh and the
factions argue about whether to have let them in. Two centuries, like Emerraine,
and nobody alive remembers a before -- the factions now argue about each other and
treat the portal as terrain.

**District count.** One district is a single point of contact and one fight. Two or
three districts (Arborisle, Tertara, Shubae) means the districts compete with one
another, and native factions get to play them off. Multi-district worlds are much
richer and much harder to run.

**The world's own weirdness.** This is where thematic consistency actually lives.
Every world has one fact that bends the standard five out of shape, and that
bending is the world's personality:

| World | The fact | What it does to factions |
|---|---|---|
| **Emerraine** | Magic-dead world; magic works only inside the portal zones | The district isn't merely rich, it's the only place a cleric gets spells. Factions organise around access to *being able to work at all* |
| **Mortuus Rex** | Civilised nations besieged by undead; the god sustaining the wards is dying | The district is a lifeboat. The only political question is who gets aboard |
| **Profugae** | Ruins of an ecumenopolis flattened by falling debris | Natives are scavenging their own ancestors' city, and the empire is a rival scavenger with better paperwork |
| **Langsevain** | Steampunk; independent technological breakthroughs | They do not need imperial technology and they know it. The only faction on the ring that negotiates from strength |
| **Arborisle** | Majority elven, three districts, one sited in the only non-elven region | An ethnic fault line the empire has already exploited once, deliberately, by choosing where to build |
| **Hrimgard** | Ice world (called Nevermelt in older notes) | Survival infrastructure is the currency. Whoever heats the town governs it |

## Generating one in five minutes

1. Pick a **position** from the five.
2. Give it a **rival** from a different position. This is the faction's actual
   politics; the empire is scenery.
3. Give it **one thing the portal took and one thing the portal gave**. Both, always.
4. Decide what it **cannot have**: citizenship, a seat, a berth, a licence, a
   name. Something specific and denied by a rule rather than by a person.
5. Name it in the **world's own naming pool**, not the imperial one. The district
   names come from the House spheres and sound Croatian, Danish, Gaelic, French,
   Basque and so on. A native faction that sounds like a district is a native
   faction that reads as imperial.

### Worked example

**The Ninefold Draught, Issoire, Shubae.** *Position: Hinterland. Rival: the
Brokers.*

The freshwater guild of the island chain feeding Issoire. Before the portal they
sold water to ships; now they sell it to a district that never sleeps and pays in
imperial coin at Imperial Time. The portal took their seasonal rhythm and gave
them more money than four generations saw combined. What they cannot have is a
berth licence in Issoire harbour, because berths are allocated by the district
Administrator of the Low City Office, and licences go to citizens.
So they sell through brokers who take a third, and they have been quietly buying
one broker family's debts for six years.

That's a faction, a rival, a grievance, a resource, and three jobs, and it took
one paragraph and no imperial politics whatsoever.

## Open, and deliberately so

- Lesser House names. None exist. Invent freely.
- Which Great Houses have fallen historically, and to whom.
- Low City non-House organisations: hybrids of High City mercantile houses and
  each world's own structures. Undefined.
- Everything beyond the Low City on every world.
