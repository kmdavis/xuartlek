# The High City Factions

Who actually holds power in the empire. How the state works is in
[[Government of Xuartlek]]; the city itself is [[The High City]]. For inventing
factions native to a Low City world, see [[Native Factions]].

## The Six Great Houses

A House becomes a **Great** House by holding one of the six Estates, and by no
other route. There is no size, wealth or age requirement. Six Estates, six Great
Houses, and the only way in is to take one.

| House | Origin | Estate | Districts held | Sphere |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Von Schoeler** | German | Anwesen | Altstadt, Neuestadt, Tiergarten | none |
| **Beauharnais** | French | Maison | Deux Rivières, Haut Jardin | Quais Ouest |
| **Rantzau** | Nordic | Ejendom | Loppemarked, Lav Have | Østre Havn |
| **Árpád** | Hungarian | Itthon | Dvorski Vrt | Sjeverna Luka |
| **Maolmhuaidh** | Scottish | Dhachaigh | Fearann Gnìomhachais | Docaichean a Deas |
| **Haritza** | Basque | Hasiera | Lapurrak, Hiltzaileak, Gezurtiak, Iruzurrak | none |

**They are not six equals.** Von Schoeler, Beauharnais and Rantzau run three
districts each; Árpád and Maolmhuaidh run two. Haritza runs four, all of them
criminal, none of them in the High City proper, and their right to representation
is openly debated in the Assize.

**They took their districts with money, not soldiers.** Buying out scute-holders,
marrying, endowing, lending and standing surety across three centuries until the
Chamber recognised what everybody already knew. Not one district changed hands by
force, which is why no boundary in the city has a line on it anywhere.

**Von Schoeler is first among equals, and holding no harbour is the reason.** They
run fleets of merchantmen and dock them in everyone else's rim districts. Owning a
harbour would tie them to one door; being the largest customer in all four makes
them indispensable to four rivals who cannot move their harbours. When Von Schoeler
shifts its business from Østre Havn to Quais Ouest, Rantzau feels it and
Beauharnais collects. Everyone else guards a door. Von Schoeler guards the middle
and rents all four.

### The distinction that matters: control versus sphere

A House **controls** its inner districts outright. It merely has a **sphere of
influence** over its rim harbour, and the four rim harbours are where every one of
the 23 Low City portals lands.

So the most valuable ground in the empire is the ground the Houses hold least
securely. Every trade dispute, every smuggling route and every succession crisis
runs through a harbour that its House influences rather than owns.

### The Estates

Each Estate rides on the shell of a **Lesser Skyy Tortle**: one of Xuartlek's six
first disciples, partially ascended as he is, and roughly an order of magnitude
smaller. Roughly 2.5 miles of shell each, against Xuartlek's 25.

> **Terminology.** Call them **Lesser Skyy Tortles**, not Ancients. "Ancients" in
> this setting means the level-80 Far Realm manifestations, and "ancient Skyy
> Tortles" means the millennia-old ones who stayed on Tortleheim. Both are
> something else entirely.

Anwesen, Maison, Ejendom, Itthon, Dhachaigh, Hasiera.

## The Lesser Houses

Probably hundreds, based out of the High City. Some barely qualify for the
capital H. A few are within shouting distance of Great House status and lack only
an Estate.

**A Great House can fall and be replaced.** It has happened once or twice. So
every Lesser House watches for the opening, and every Great House watches its
back, permanently. Most mid-sized Lesser Houses and a good number of the large
ones are vassals of one Great House or another, which means a Great House's
gravest threat is usually one of its own clients.

None are named yet. Name them freely.

## The Sovereign Institutions

Four High City districts sit outside House politics, held by institutions rather
than families.

| Institution | District |
|---|---|
| The Academy of Arcane Sciences | Akademinin Evi |
| The Imperial Military | Karinis Kvartal |
| The banking guilds | Pankkitoiminta |
| The shipwrights | Maestri d'Ascia |

Two notes worth having:

**The banking guilds are plural.** Pankkitoiminta is run by a cartel that agrees
on very little except that no House should ever hold it.

**The Imperial Military is 110 years old.** It was formally established in 390 XI,
the year the Wars of the Dale ended, which means the empire fought that war with
constables and improvisation and lost a world doing it. It is the youngest
institution in a 500-year-old city.

**The Academy is the oldest**, older than the portals, the Houses and the Authority.
It formed on Furitas during the 045-075 stay and built the first portal in 090,
fifteen years too late to keep it.

Those two are co-equal with the six Houses. The Houses accumulated; the Academy and
the military were each forged by a lost world, which is why they have more in
common with each other than either has with a House. The banking guilds and the
shipwrights hold their districts by occupation and have never been recognised as
holding anything. See [[Government of Xuartlek]] for how that works and why the banks cannot
unify.

## The State

Full treatment in [[Government of Xuartlek]]. Four bodies:

**Xuartlek** is head of state and silent.

**The Assize** holds fifty seats, one per district: 17 High City + 6 Estate + 4
Under City + 23 Low City. Members are **Delegates**. Its only power is
**recognition**, because it descends unbroken from a pilgrim council of scute
elders that settled boundary disputes and never acquired a second function.

**The Authority** (formally the Portal Authority) is the executive, operating under
licence from the Assize. Its only enforcement power is refusal of passage, and it is
funded **entirely by tolls**. No head tax, no property tax, no sales tax, because
the founding council had no power to levy one and told the Authority to fund itself
instead.

**The Assay** tries. Split off from the Assize in 390 and constituted by the Concord
itself, which is why it is genuinely independent: a licence can be reviewed, a
treaty clause cannot be amended without reopening every boundary in the city.

**The military** sits outside all three, on a first charge against the tolls.

## The Authority's four departments

The most useful faction material at the table, because a department always has a
local officer with a name and a grievance. Each sits under a **Comptroller**; the
Comptrollers sit as **the Board** in the **Counting House**, longest-serving as
**First Comptroller**.

| Department | What it means for a GM |
|---|---|
| **Transit** | Every toll, fee and penalty. Portals primary **and** secondary, harbours, trams, the Air Guard. This is the entire revenue of the state |
| **the Register** | Executes recognition. Citizens, titles, hulls, boundaries. The quietest and most dangerous department in a state built on recognition |
| **the Low City** | Administers **every Low City district** through a named Administrator with a Guard, a Services and an Infrastructure deputy. Your default imperial authority on any world |
| **the Mint** | Coin, weights, Imperial Time |

**The Comptroller of Transit is the most powerful official in the city**, because
the other three spend money that one collects, and almost no Delegate could name
them.

## Law and enforcement

**Every district has its own constabulary**, financed by whoever controls the
district and required by law to enforce the decrees of the Assay. Fifty districts,
fifty forces, fifty names, and **none with jurisdiction across a district line.**

So a constabulary is routinely ordered to act against the House that pays its wages,
and anything that crosses a boundary either dies or reaches the Assay. If the
constabulary fails, the military is also bound by the decree; if the matter is too
sensitive for either, the Assay has four enforcers of its own. Compliance runs on
embarrassment rather than force. Details, including the four, in [[Government of Xuartlek]].

**The Under City sinecure is a plot on its own.** Administrators are appointed to
Lapurrak, Hiltzaileak, Gezurtiak and Iruzurrak and the posts do nothing at all.
Someone holds those four jobs. Somebody's nephew.

## Citizenship, and why it generates most of your conflict

Citizenship comes three ways: **born to citizens, earned by military service, or
purchased.**

Land ownership in the High City, the Under City and the Estates **requires** it.
Non-citizens **can** own land in the Low City.

The consequences do most of your worldbuilding for you:

- A Low City district can be economically dominated by non-citizens who cannot
  vote in it, hold office in it, or own ground anywhere that matters.
- That district still returns one member to the chamber, elected by whichever
  minority holds the franchise.
- The Low City's 23 seats now outnumber the High City and Under City's 21. Only
  the six Estate seats keep the core territories at 27-23.
- **Military service is the naturalisation pipeline**, which is why the Imperial
  Military holds a district of its own and why enlistment means something very
  different on Emerraine than it does in Altstadt.
- Wealth can buy citizenship, so every rich non-citizen family on every world is
  one transaction away from a vote, and everyone already enfranchised knows it.

This is the fault line the next few decades of imperial history run along, and it
is entirely absent from the party's job description, which is exactly where it
should stay.

The oldest inherited citizenship traces to the founding congregation, which was
entirely Skyy Tortle. See [[Government of Xuartlek]].

## The Under City

Four districts, House Haritza, and several organised crime syndicates, roughly
half of them connected to Haritza or one of its clients. The other half is the
interesting half.

The research body holding the Niflheim portal allocation is chartered by Haritza and
is 215 years older than its current directors.
