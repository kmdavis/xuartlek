# Government

Who actually holds power on Xuartlek, why the state is so weak, and how it got
that way. Factions and the power map are in [[factions]].

---

## The shape of it, in one paragraph

Nobody designed this state. A congregation of pilgrims built a neighbourhood
council to settle boundary disputes; that council chartered somebody else to run
the portals because it had no power to do anything itself; and four hundred years
later that somebody administers a city as a side effect of collecting tolls.
**The Assize can only recognise. The Authority can only refuse passage.** The
Assay can try you, and the army will come if the Assay says so, and both of those
exist because a war went badly in 390.

Everything else in the city belongs to eight powers who are not part of the
government at all.

## The four bodies

| Body | Founded | Holds its power from | Can |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the Assize** | 000, as the Council of Scutes | Nothing. It simply is | Recognise |
| **the Authority** | 105, the Charter | **A licence from the Assize** | Refuse passage, and collect |
| **the Assay** | 390, the Concord | A clause in the treaty | Try, and be enforced |
| **the military** | 390, the Concord | A clause, and a first charge on the tolls | Be called by the Assay |

Two ancient bodies and two war-children, and the war-children are the ones nobody
can touch. A licence can be reviewed. A treaty clause cannot be amended without
reopening every soft boundary in the city, which no House will ever risk.

# Constitutional history

## Era 1: the Council of Scutes (000-105)

**The founding population was entirely Skyy Tortle**, and they came as
**pilgrims**. Not refugees. A few thousand who chose to follow a god who had
already ascended, settling the central scutes of a twenty-five mile shell,
speaking Tortlian, organised by whoever could organise a settlement.

What they built was the **Council of Scutes**: one elder for each settled scute.
Its entire business was deciding who could build where, whose claim ran to which
ridge, and whose word counted as title.

**That body is the Assize, and it never acquired a second function.**

This is why the government is weak, and it is not decay. It is a residents'
association that was never given anything else to do. Five centuries on it still
performs the one act it was invented for: **it recognises.** Boundaries first,
then titles, eventually persons. The thirteen inner districts are the thirteen
natural scutes, and district representation descends unbroken from thirteen elders
arguing about a ridge line.

The Assize still conducts recognition in **Tortlian**, which most of the city no
longer speaks. Recognition is a ritual act performed in a dead tongue by the
descendants of people who no longer own the city.

## Era 2: the Charter, and the Portal Authority (090-125)

The Academy had formed on **Furitas** during the 045-075 stay, and Furitas was a
high-magic world. Xuartlek left in 075. **The Academy built the first portal in
090**, fifteen years too late to keep the best home it will ever have had.
Everything the Academy has done since is an answer to arriving late.

Renestrae's portal completes in 100. The Council has no mechanism for this at all,
because you cannot recognise your way to operating a door.

Then **105: the accident.** Improper handling, inadequate protocols, a portal
lost. That is the founding crisis of the executive, and it happens before a single
Great House exists.

The Council's answer is the only one available to a body that can only recognise:
it **charters** somebody else. A **Portal Authority**, with a narrow remit,
permanent Academy involvement as the technical authority, and no power beyond the
door.

> **The toll exists because the founding council had no power to tax.**

They could not levy a rate on the scutes. They had never had that power and asking
for it would have ended the Council. So the Authority was told to fund itself from
the only thing it controlled. Four centuries of fiscal policy is a workaround by a
residents' association that could not collect subs, and every absurdity in the
modern fee schedule descends from it. A body barred from taxing income, property
or sales has exactly one instrument, and it has had four hundred years to refine
it.

**And everything else accreted onto it, because it was the only part of the state
with money.** The Register, the Low City administration, the Mint: none could fund
themselves and the Authority could. Today it administers a city, and its formal
name is still about portals.

## Era 3: the trade centuries (125-370)

**120 is when the High City becomes a monopoly. 125 is when it stops being one.**

With Athaneli's portal open there are two doors, and every journey between worlds
passes through the shell and pays. Five years later the Renestrae-Athaneli link
opens the first route that does not. There are now 22 secondary portals in two
interlocking rings.

**The Authority tolls the secondaries too.** So it is indifferent to where traffic
flows as long as it flows through a door, and the Houses are not indifferent at
all, because secondary traffic never touches a rim harbour. For four centuries the
Authority has had every incentive to build secondaries and the Houses every
incentive to block them, and the Authority has won twenty-two times.

### Where everyone else came from

Every non-tortle in the city arrived through a portal the tortles built. Elves from
Arborisle after 100, then Profugae, then every world after, and they did not settle
in tidy national quarters. They settled wherever there was room. **The city is
literally cosmopolitan and always has been.**

### Why the district names are a hodgepodge

Because a district's name is a **recognition**, and recognition is a vote.

When a district needs a name, anybody may propose one, in any language they like,
including Tortlian. The proposals are read, argued over, mocked, and one is adopted
by the Assize. A typical sitting produces something dignified, something
incomprehensible, something with a joke buried in it, and one entry from whoever is
annoyed that day:

> *District Seven. Proposals before the Assize: Schellengmacher. Fibonacci della
> Armistraccia. The Gardens. Zorgathul. Number Seven, And Let Us Please Move On.*

So **a district's name tells you nothing about who lives there or what they speak.**
Maestri d'Ascia does not mean the shipwrights are Italian. It means somebody
proposed an Italian name four hundred years ago and it carried the room.

The rejected proposals are all still on file in the Register, because nothing in
this city is ever thrown away, and they are among the funniest documents in the
empire.

**The one safe inference is age.** Anything named before about 100 is Tortlian,
because there was nobody else in the room to propose anything else. Everything after
that is whatever won a vote.

### A House is a partnership, not a bloodline

**A House is a chartered partnership recognised by the Assize.** Members are not
required to be related, though they usually are, and a good number of Houses are
three or four genetic families who merged generations ago and never unwound it.

This is the single most useful fact about House politics:

- **It explains the naming.** A House takes the name of its principal district
  precisely because it is not one family and cannot take a family name.
- **A Great House falling and being replaced is a merger or an acquisition**, not
  a war. Which fits, because not one district ever changed hands by force.
- **Upward mobility is a business deal.** A Lesser House rises by acquiring a
  component of a Great House, or by outbidding one for a defecting partner.
- **Internal House disputes are justiciable.** A charter is a legal instrument, so
  a House's private quarrels can end up in front of the Assay.
- **Nothing stops a component defecting** except a charter recognised by a body
  where the Low City now holds 23 of 50 seats.
- **Several Houses have tortle components**, who sold in generations ago. The
  founders became minority partners in their own city.

### How the Houses took their districts

**With money.** Buying out scute-holders, marrying, endowing, lending, standing
surety, until the Assize recognised what everybody already knew.

That is worse than conquest, and it is why the borders are soft. A boundary
assembled from three hundred years of purchases and marriages has no line on it
anywhere.

The Estates became the marker of arrival for one reason: there are six, and there
will only ever be six. A fixed-supply status good, like a seat on an exchange.

## Era 4: the Concord (390)

The Wars of the Dale break all of it. No army. The district constabularies fail.
Duathamper is severed in 384 and dead by 386, two years on the wrong side of a door
its own side closed.

The settlement of 390 rewrites the 105 Charter:

| Who | What they got |
|---|---|
| **The military** | Created. Funded by a **first charge on the tolls**, fixed in the Concord, outside House command |
| **The Assay** | **Split off from the Assize.** The war proved fifty Delegates cannot try a Great House, so the trying half was cut out and constituted by treaty rather than by licence |
| **The Houses** | The six Estates **guaranteed in perpetuity**. Their price for accepting an army they would not control |
| **The Houses again** | Boundaries frozen **"as they lie"**, deliberately unsurveyed, because surveying them in 390 would have restarted the war at home |
| **The Academy** | Technical authority over portals, rings and anchors, written in permanently |
| **The Low City** | **Seats in the Assize**, conceded because the war needed colonial money and colonial bodies |
| **Everyone** | **Citizenship by military service**, for the same reason |
| **Nobody** | A strong government. That was the thing they were all afraid of |

Three consequences still running:

**The nebulous borders are a treaty clause.** Vague because in 390 nobody dared
draw them, and reopening the Concord to draw them reopens everything else.

**The Low City concession is killing the Houses on a slow fuse.** They bought
wartime loyalty with seats. Those seats are now 23 of 50.

**The military's contempt has a precise object.** They watched a city die for two
years, and the treaty that followed guaranteed six manor houses riding on the backs
of animals and refused to draw a single line on a map. They do not want an Estate.
They think the Estates are the disease, and they can cite the clause.

# The state today

## Xuartlek

Head of state. Silent. Every authority below him is delegated, and delegation from
a silent god can never be confirmed, so no institution can prove it holds the last
word. That is the structural reason nobody consolidates.

## The Assize

Fifty seats, one per district: 17 High City + 6 Estate + 4 Under City + 23 Low
City. Members are **Delegates**.

**Its only power is recognition.** It cannot tax, raise troops, seize property, or
compel a House. It can only say that a thing is so: who holds an Estate, where a
boundary runs, who is a citizen, whose title to a hull is good. It also **licenses
the Authority**, which is the one lever it holds over the executive and the one it
has never dared pull.

The Houses tolerate it because they took their districts with money, and what money
cannot buy is legitimacy. Recognition is the one commodity every House needs and no
House can seize.

It is why the Low City's 23 seats are frightening. Recognition is a vote, and on any
recognition question the Low City is four seats from a majority.

## The Authority

Formally the Portal Authority. Nobody uses the long form.

Departments under **Comptrollers**, sitting as **the Board** in the **Counting
House**, the longest-serving as **First Comptroller** and de facto head of
government.

| Department | Remit |
|---|---|
| **Transit** | Every toll, fee and penalty. Portals primary **and** secondary, harbours, trams, the Air Guard. **This is the entire revenue of the state** |
| **the Register** | Executes recognition. Citizens, titles, hulls, boundaries |
| **the Low City** | Administers the 23 colonial districts through named Administrators, each with a Guard, a Services and an Infrastructure deputy |
| **the Mint** | Coin, weights, Imperial Time |

**The Comptroller of Transit is the most powerful official in the city**, because
the other three departments spend money that one department collects. They are a
functionary, not a politician, and most Delegates could not name them.

**The Authority's only enforcement power is refusal of passage.** It cannot arrest,
tax or seize except at a door. But declining to open a door to a House's cargo is
economic warfare conducted with a ledger.

### Revenue

**No head tax. No property tax. No sales tax.**

- Portal transit, primary and secondary, flat by vessel class
- Harbour dues at the four rim harbours and every Low City port
- The four hanging bridges out of the Under City
- Trams
- Vessel transfer duties, registry fees, and the entire apparatus of penalty

So **the Houses pay almost nothing directly.** They pay when they move things. A
House that trades pays; a House that sits still does not.

**And the harbours are the sore point.** Every Low City portal lands in a rim
harbour inside a House's sphere and the Authority collects there, not the House.
Árpád's harbour is the busiest asset they have and they see none of it. That is
exactly why the four harbours are *sphere of influence* rather than *control*:
constitutionally shared ground, resented by everybody.

## The Assay

Constituted by the Concord in 390, split off from the Assize because fifty Delegates
cannot try a House. Independent, because its authority is a treaty clause and not a
licence.

### The constabularies, and enforcement by humiliation

**Every district, High and Low, has its own constabulary.** Fifty districts, fifty
forces, fifty names in fifty languages. Each is financed by whoever controls the
district, and each is **required by law to enforce the decrees of the Assay.**

**No constabulary has jurisdiction across a district line.** Fifty small forces that
cannot pursue, which is why anything crossing a boundary either dies or reaches the
Assay.

Three rungs, and each is more humiliating than the last:

1. **The district constabulary.** Which is routinely required to enforce a decree
   against the House that pays its wages. This is the standing drama of the setting
   and no GM has to invent it.
2. **The military**, also required by law, and acting only on a decree, never on its
   own motion. If soldiers enter your district, your House has been publicly shamed.
3. **The Assay's own enforcers.** Few, and reserved for sensitive matters, meaning
   cases against a House or against a constabulary itself.

**Compliance in this city runs on embarrassment rather than force.** Houses enforce
decrees against themselves rather than let it escalate, which is how a weak state
gets obeyed.

### The four wards

The Assay's own enforcers number four, and they are all Skyy Tortles, and they are
all technically still awaiting trial.

They were **orphans and juvenile delinquents**, arraigned and awaiting a hearing,
when an unlicensed portal opening in the Under City altered them. Their growth
stopped and their minds did not decline: the condition Xuartlek spent a century of
study and a failed ascension trying to achieve, delivered by an industrial accident
to four adolescents who had not asked for it. They came out of it permanently young,
permanently sharp, and, for reasons nobody has satisfactorily explained, with a
conscience.

**The arraignment was never resolved.** The Assay made them wards without disposing
of the case, so their guardian is also their judge and the hearing can be convened
at any time. They serve a court that has never stopped being able to try them.

**They are also the only physical evidence** that ever came out of the Under City
opening. By the time investigators arrived the thing was closed and disclaimed, and
the case has been open and unproven for their whole lives. That is the real reason
they will never be discharged.

**They were named by the ysoki archivist** who signed the custody form because
nobody else would, after four judges of the Assay's first century. He has explained
who those judges were on many occasions. None of the four has ever listened.

| Named for | Remembered as |
|---|---|
| **Irnerio** | Recovered the pre-Concord recognitions that everyone had assumed lost, and proved the Assize's rulings ran unbroken back to the scute elders. They called him the lamp |
| **Accursio** | Compiled four centuries of those recognitions into one usable commentary. The reason anybody can read the law at all |
| **Bartolo** | Worked out **which law applies when a case crosses a district line**. Every cross-boundary prosecution since rests on him |
| **Gentili** | Wrote the law of what an army may do, in the years after the war that made one. Then took the Under City cases, and quietly stopped being anywhere |

Recovered, organised, extended, killed. The archivist chose that fourth name on
purpose.

A striking number of the Assay's early bench came out of **Maestri d'Ascia**, which
people find significant and which is almost certainly an accident of who was
available in 390. The irony stands either way: the court that recognises things was
built largely by people from the one district whose own occupancy has never been
recognised.

> Named for Italian jurists: Irnerius (c. 1050-1125), Accursius (c. 1182-1263),
> Bartolus de Saxoferrato (1313-1357) and Alberico Gentili (1552-1608). Dates from
> memory; check before they reach a handout. The real Bartolus founded conflict of
> laws, which is precisely this city's foundational legal problem, and the real
> Irnerius revived Roman law from near-oblivion and was called *lucerna juris*.

## The military

**Its money is a first charge on the tolls, fixed by the Concord.** Not a
subscription anyone renews. It comes off the top before the Authority pays for
anything.

Restraints, all structural rather than moral:

- **It cannot complete a citizenship without the Assize.** It can enlist anyone; the
  franchise at the end must be recognised. Its recruiting pipeline depends on the one
  body it could most easily overthrow.
- **It has no revenue beyond the charge** and cannot raise one.
- **It acts domestically only on an Assay decree**, never on its own motion.
- **Duathamper.** Its founding act was closing a door on its own people and listening
  to them die. Turning on the citizenry would make it the thing it exists to prevent.

Holds Karinis Kvartal outright, not as a sphere, and returns a Delegate.

## The Academy

Co-equal without an Estate or a soldier, because in a state whose entire revenue is
tolls on portal transit, **the Academy is the institution that understands portals.**
Anchors, rings, shear, gauge, maintenance, and the airship levitation rings and the
exploration rings besides.

Transit collects the money. The Academy keeps the doors working. If they stopped,
the revenue would stop, and no amount of coin or soldiers would restart it. A veto
nobody voted for and nobody can remove. They have never threatened to use it, which
everyone finds more unnerving than a threat.

Oldest institution in the city bar the Assize: it predates the portals, the Houses
and the Authority.

## The banks and the wrights

Both hold their districts by **occupation**, and under a recognition-based
constitution that means their title has never been recognised. They have no legal
claim to Pankkitoiminta or Maestri d'Ascia. They are simply there.

**Both know perfectly well that organising would make them co-equal with any House.**
There is a standing committee. It has met for roughly two hundred years. It convenes,
it agrees unanimously and with real enthusiasm that unification would be
transformative, it passes a resolution, and it adjourns. Nothing happens. Everyone
goes home pleased.

They are all extremely comfortable. What they say in public and what they do are not
the same thing and have not been for two centuries. The minutes are an unbroken
archive of unanimous agreement and zero action.

**On the rare occasion somebody actually pushes it forward, they are bribed by a
House, or they quietly stop.** The Houses are jealous of that power and much prefer
it stayed potential.

The shipwrights run the same committee less obnoxiously, because a few of them will
admit over a drink that they don't mean it either.

## Citizenship

Born to citizens, earned by military service, or purchased. Land ownership in the
High City, Under City and Estates requires it. Non-citizens **can** own land in the
Low City. The oldest inherited citizenship traces to the founding congregation, which
was entirely Skyy Tortle.

- A Low City district can be economically dominated by non-citizens who cannot vote
  in it, hold office in it, or own ground anywhere that matters, and it still returns
  a Delegate elected by whichever minority holds the franchise.
- Military service is the naturalisation pipeline, dated to the Concord.
- Wealth buys citizenship, so every rich non-citizen family on every world is one
  transaction from a vote, and everyone already enfranchised knows it.

## The tortles now

Roughly 100,000 of a million. They hold no Estate. They hold **several Delegate
seats**, they sit as **junior partners inside a number of Houses**, and above all
they are the permanent bureaucracy: the Authority's departments are old, conduct
their business in Tortlian, and hire on continuity.

So the political state is migrant and the permanent state is tortle. The Comptroller
of Transit, the most powerful official in the city, is almost certainly a tortle
nobody outside the Counting House has heard of.

## Who actually holds power

| Tier | Who |
|---|---|
| **Head of state** | Xuartlek. Silent |
| **Eight co-equals** | Von Schoeler, Beauharnais, Rantzau, Árpád, Maolmhuaidh, Haritza, the military, the Academy |
| **In possession, unrecognised** | the banking guilds, the shipwrights |
| **The arena** | the Assize. Fifty Delegates. Can only recognise |
| **The apparatus** | the Authority. Four departments. Can only refuse passage |
| **The teeth** | the Assay, and whoever it orders to act |

**Von Schoeler holds no rim harbour and that is the source of their primacy.** They
run fleets of merchantmen and dock them in everyone else's harbours. Owning a harbour
would tie them to one door; being the largest customer in all four makes them
indispensable to four rivals who cannot move their harbours. When Von Schoeler shifts
its business from Østre Havn to Quais Ouest, Rantzau feels it and Beauharnais
collects.

## Standing tensions, ready to use

1. **The Authority builds secondary portals. The Houses' harbours starve.** Four
   centuries old, purely economic, always live.
2. **The Low City is four seats from controlling recognition.**
3. **A constabulary is required to enforce a decree against the House that pays it.**
4. **Nobody dares request a boundary recognition**, because the Assize would then look
   at all your edges.
5. **The banks agree with themselves every few decades and do nothing**, and the ones
   who push either take the money or stop being anywhere.
6. **The Academy could end the state's income by downing tools** and never mentions it.
7. **The Assay has wanted the Under City opening for a century** and has four pieces of
   evidence that grew up.

- What happened to Gentili.
